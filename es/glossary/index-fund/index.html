index fund

Finanzas

A fund that holds the securities in a market index, in the index's proportions, instead of choosing among them. A total US stock market fund holds nearly every listed US company, each in proportion to its market value. Its return follows the market it tracks, less its cost, so it will never beat that market. What it offers instead is breadth (diversification across thousands of holdings), a low expense ratio, and low turnover. Because an index fund rarely sells, it realizes few gains, and in a taxable account it tends to pay few capital-gain distributions. Index exchange-traded funds usually pay even fewer. Index funds and actively managed funds are both reasonable choices. An index fund accepts the market's return at a low cost; an active fund pays a manager to try for something different.
