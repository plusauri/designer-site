longevity risk

Finanzas

The chance of living longer than the money lasts. A plan has to pay for every year a person is alive, and nobody knows in advance how many years that is. A 65-year-old couple has a good chance that one of them reaches 90, and a real chance that one reaches 95 or beyond. Planning to an average lifespan means running short in about half of the futures where the plan matters most. The risk is largest for a single person who pays for retirement from savings alone, because nothing else keeps paying. It is also large for someone whose lifetime income is a flat amount: a pension with no cost-of-living raise lasts for life, but it buys less every year. Income that lasts for life and rises with prices is the usual protection, which is why delaying Social Security and buying an inflation-adjusted annuity are both described as longevity insurance. In Tutilis, each person's death age is set under People, and Transforms' Retirement & forecast end moves it for a what-if, so you can see what living to 100 does to a plan.

Dónde está en la aplicación

Dónde se ingresa

  • Each person's death age; the plan pays for every year up to it.

Dónde probarlo como escenario hipotético

  • Move a death age later, such as to 100, and compare the plan with and without it.
