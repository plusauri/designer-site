credit risk

Finanzas

The chance that a borrower pays late, pays less than promised, or stops paying. It is the risk a lender takes that the money will not come back as agreed. US Treasuries are treated as free of it, because the federal government borrows in the currency it issues. Every other bond carries some. Rating agencies grade it, and a lower grade pays a higher yield to make up for it. A high-yield bond is one graded below investment grade. A municipal bond has credit risk of its own kind. A general-obligation bond is backed by a government's power to tax. A revenue bond is paid from what one project earns (a toll road's tolls, a hospital's fees, a utility's bills), and it is only as good as that revenue. Defaults on rated municipal bonds have been rare, but they happen: Detroit's 2013 bankruptcy cut payments to some of its bondholders, and Puerto Rico restructured most of its debt after 2017. A household that buys bonds from its own state for the state tax break also ties more of its money to that one state's finances. In a Tutilis forecast, a bond category carries price swings. Defaults are not modeled as events.
