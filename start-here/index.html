Planning can feel like a wall of choices. This map shows the decisions that are yours to make, with the ones most people think about first near the top. Open a step to see its questions and the choices behind them.

0What does making it mean to you?Worth a look1 question

This changes the scoreboard rather than the plan, and every later "is this working?" depends on it.

  • What would "enough" look like?

    • “Making it means never running out before 95.”
    • “Making it means leaving the house to the kids.”

    Decide · Always open

  1. 1Steady groundWorth a look3 questions

    • Do you have a cash cushion for essential spending?If: No

      • “I build a cash cushion.”

      👍 Many people aim for three to six months of essential spending.

      Decide · Always open; cheapest before trouble arrives

    • Do you carry debt at a high rate, such as credit cards or most personal loans?If: Yes

      • “I pay down the highest-rate debt.”

      Act · Always open

    • Do you know where the money goes each month?If: No

      • “I write down what we spend for one month.”

      Decide · Always open

    Lessons:A budget checklist for a first paycheck
  2. 2Free money and cheap winsWorth a look2 questions

    Fees are here because the levers grading found the advisor fee to be the highest cost-adjusted lever: real reach, near-zero cost to the household's life.

    • Does your employer match retirement contributions?If: Yes

      • “I contribute at least enough to get the full match.”

      Decide · Open while employed

    • What do you pay in fees, all in, for advice and funds?If: More than you expected

      • “I move to lower-cost funds.”
      • “I renegotiate or replace my advisor.”

      👍 All-in fees above about 0.5% a year are worth a look.

      Decide · Always open; compounds, so strongest early

    Lessons:Cost and benefit of an advisor
  3. 3How much, and into which bucketWorth a look4 questions

    • How much of each paycheck do you save?

      • “I save more.”
      • “I raise my savings rate one point a year.”

      Decide · Strongest in the 20s to 40s, fades after 55

    • What do you spend, and could you live on less?

      • “We live on less.”

      Decide · Always open; the most powerful act and the most painful

    • Pay tax now or later?

      • “I put this year's savings in a Roth.”
      • “I put this year's savings in a pre-tax account.”

      Decide · Open while earning

    • Saving for a child?If: Yes

      • “I open a 529 or a Trump Account.”

      Act · Closes as the child nears 18

    Lessons:A Trump Account for a newborn
  4. 4The big-ticket choicesWorth a look5 questions

    The large, infrequent decisions that most budgets are made of.

    • Is the house the right size and cost?

      • “We downsize.”
      • “I refinance.”
      • “I take a 15-year mortgage instead of a 30.”

      Act · Open, with transaction friction

    • Where do you live?

      • “We move to a lower-tax state.”

      Act · Open; easiest at retirement

    • College?

      • “We choose a cheaper school.”
      • “We plan a gap year.”

      Act · Closes when the child enrolls

    • Cars and other recurring replacements?

      • “I keep the car three years longer.”

      Act · Always open

    • Could an asset earn?

      • “I rent out the spare unit.”

      Act · Open while you own it

  5. 5When you stopWorth a look5 questions

    Retirement timing is the headline lever.

    • When do you stop working?

      • “I work three more years.”
      • “I retire at 60.”

      Decide · The biggest lever at 45; nearly closed at 70

    • Will you stay in this house and this state?

      • “We downsize.”
      • “We move to a lower-tax state.”

      ↩ Also on rung 4

      Act · Strongest at retirement

    • Could you stop gradually?

      • “I go part time for five years.”

      Act · Open until you stop

    • Stopping before 59½?If: Yes

      • “I plan which accounts I draw from until 59½, such as the rule of 55 or a taxable bridge.”

      Decide · Closes at 59½

    • Stopping before 65?If: Yes

      • “I price marketplace health coverage for the gap years.”

      Act · Closes at 65

    Lessons:The five-year retirement runwayGetting safer as retirement approachesGlide paths through retirement
  6. 6Turning savings into a paycheckWorth a look5 questions

    • When do you claim Social Security?

      • “I claim at 70 instead of 62.”

      Decide · Open 62 to 70; closes for good at 70

    • When does a pension start?

      • “I start it later.”

      Decide · Plan-specific

    • Which accounts do you draw from first?

      • “I draw taxable first, then pre-tax, then Roth.”

      Decide · Open through retirement

    • Is there a low-tax window before Social Security and RMDs?

      • “I convert some pre-tax savings to Roth in the low-income years.”

      Decide · Opens when paychecks stop, closes at RMD age

    • How much do you take each year?

      • “I follow a rule and write down when I will cut back.”

      Decide · Open through retirement

    Lessons:Claim early and invest, or wait?
  7. 7A floor under the essentialsWorth a look1 question

    • Does income that arrives regardless of markets cover your essential spending?If: No

      • “I buy an annuity sized to close the gap.”
      • “I set aside a bond ladder for the gap years.”

      Act · Best priced in the late 60s to 70s

    • Does income that arrives regardless of markets cover your essential spending?If: Yes

      • “I treat the portfolio as the discretionary budget.”

      Decide · Open through retirement
