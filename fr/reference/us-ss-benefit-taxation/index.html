Taxation of Social Security benefits

The IRC §86 provisional-income test that decides whether 0%, up to 50%, or up to 85% of benefits is taxable.

Nominal

Source: Publication 915 — Social Security and Equivalent Railroad Retirement Benefits — Internal Revenue Service

https://www.irs.gov/publications/p915

Where we use this · Used in your forecast

  • Decides how much of each year's Social Security benefit is added to ordinary income on the federal tax statement — none, up to half, or up to 85%.
  • Because the thresholds are held nominal, a benefit that grows with inflation pulls progressively more of itself into tax over a long retirement.

Not modeled

Tax-exempt interest is not modeled as a separate bucket, so provisional income uses zero for it. Thresholds are held nominal — Congress never indexed them.

Thresholds are statutory dollars Congress never indexed. Holding them nominal is the honest long-run result — a growing benefit pulls progressively more of itself into tax (the "tax torpedo").
