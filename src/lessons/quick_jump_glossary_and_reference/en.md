# Quick Jump: find anything by its name

The Learning Center is meant to be useful to everyone who visits it, whether or not Tutilis ever becomes part of how you plan. The [Glossary](/glossary) and the [Reference](/reference) are a large part of that. Both are free to read on this site, and both are inside the app as well, a keystroke away from whatever you are doing. The same keystroke opens Quick Jump, which lets you find any setting or result by name.

Every planner eventually knows the name of what they want without knowing where it lives. You may know you want the [[term:ten-year-rule|ten-year rule]], a [[term:roth-conversion|Roth conversion]] or [[term:irmaa|IRMAA]], but a plan has hundreds of inputs, what-if controls and forecast views, and the one that answers your question can be several clicks deep. This lesson shows how **Quick Jump** takes you there by name, and how the Glossary and the Reference explain the word and the figures behind it once you arrive.

The [[term:canvas]] holds Jordan, the heir from [Inheriting an IRA: the 10-year rule](/learn/inherited_ira_ten_year_rule), who is drawing an [[term:inherited-traditional-ira|inherited traditional IRA]] at the minimum required. It is a good plan for practicing, because the two questions people most often ask about it are both questions of where to look.

## Jump by name

Quick Jump is the search box at the top of the window, and it finds places rather than documents. Press **[[key:Search]]** from anywhere, including while you are typing in a field, and the caret moves into the box. Type a word such as roth, irmaa or 529, and the box lists the places in the app where that word is entered, tried as a what-if, or shown in the forecast.

[[img:quick-jump-crash]]

The picture shows the list for crash. Two words match, and each lists a what-if row in Transforms and the Realized returns<!-- term-skip: realized-gain — the name of a chart view, not a realized gain --> view of the Balance Sheet chart, so a market crash can be tried on the plan and then seen in the forecast.

The list keeps those three kinds of place in that order under each word. A place where you enter a value comes first, then a row in [[nav:transforms]] where you can try it as a what-if without changing the plan, and then the tables and charts where it shows up in the forecast. Use the arrow keys to pick a place and Enter to go there, or click it. The last row under every word opens that word in the Glossary, because a word you want to find is often a word you also want explained.

Quick Jump remembers the word after you jump. A word often lives in several places, so the next one is **[[key:Search]]** and the down arrow away, with no retyping. Press Esc to close the list, and Esc again to clear the box.

## Where an IRA drains

The ten-year rule says that most heirs must empty an inherited [[term:ira|IRA]] by the end of the tenth year after the owner's death. The question that follows is where the forecast shows that it actually happens. Type ten-year into Quick Jump and the app answers it with three places. The card below shows the two that the canvas beside this lesson can open.

[[jump:ten-year-rule]]

The first place, which only the desktop app opens, is the account itself in the Dossier, where the owner's year of death sets the deadline. The second is the Why column of the [[nav:si-table]], where each required withdrawal is marked as an [[term:rmd|RMD]]. Click the scale icon on one of those rows and a small card shows the rule that applies, the deadline year, the prior balance and the amount the year requires. The third place is the [[nav:bs-chart]], which is where the answer to the question is easiest to see. Jordan's inherited IRA is still large at the end of 2034, falls to zero at the end of 2035, the deadline year, and stays at zero after that.

## Where a conversion shows

A Roth conversion moves money from a traditional account into a [[term:roth|Roth]] account and pays the tax now. It is a plan assumption rather than an account, so its setting and its results live in different parts of the app. Type roth into Quick Jump and the word Roth conversion lists them together.

[[jump:roth-conversion]]

You turn conversions on under **Roth conversion** in [[nav:assumptions]], on the short page as well as the full panel. Once a policy is on, each year's conversion appears in the [[nav:si-table]] with Roth conversion in its Why column. The [[nav:brackets]] card shows the policy and how far each year's conversion fills the [[term:bracket|bracket]] you chose. The [[nav:cliffs]] card flags the conversion years on its path, and its IRMAA lane shows when a conversion's income lands in the two-year lookback that sets a [[term:surcharge|surcharge]] on the Medicare [[term:premium|premium]]. The federal and state statements in the [[nav:taxes]] card show the tax itself.

Jordan's plan has conversions turned off, so the Brackets card shows the policy as off and the conversion rows are empty. The places are the same on your own plan, and that is the point of jumping by name. You do not need to remember that the IRMAA effect lives on a card called Cliffs, because the word takes you there.

## The Glossary

The Glossary explains what the words in a plan mean, from [[term:agi|AGI]] to the ten-year rule. Press **[[key:Glossary]]** to open it. The key works while you are typing in a field, because the moment you most want a definition is when the caret sits in the field that used the word, and closing the field to look it up would lose your place.

Each Glossary entry ends with a block called **Where it is in the app**. It lists the same places Quick Jump lists, drawn the same way, and each one is a click that takes you there. Quick Jump is that block moved to the top of the window, where people look first. The Glossary is where you go when you want the meaning as well as the place.

The Glossary is also on this site at [/glossary](/glossary), free for anyone to read, whether or not they use the app.

## The Reference

The Reference lists every table, rate and rule the forecast runs on, such as the tax brackets, the contribution limits, the IRMAA tiers and the life-expectancy tables behind required distributions. Each one names its source and its limits. Press **[[key:Reference]]** to open it, and like the Glossary it opens while you are typing.

The Reference and the Glossary look alike on purpose, because they are the same kind of thing. The Glossary holds the vocabulary and the Reference holds the numbers. The Reference is read-only, since it records what the world publishes rather than a choice in your plan. When a forecast surprises you, the Reference is where you check which figure it used.

The Reference is on this site too, at [/reference](/reference), with the same free access as the Glossary.

## The same keys here

The canvas beside this lesson is the app itself, so Quick Jump, the Glossary and the Reference all work in it. On a Mac the keys are the same Ctrl keys as in the app. On Windows and Linux the browser already uses the Ctrl-letter keys for its own commands, so the Learning Center takes the same letters with **Alt** instead, such as [[key:Alt+Glossary]] for the Glossary.

The **Jump to** cards in other lessons are Quick Jump inside the page. Each card lists a word's places, and a click takes the canvas there, exactly as a row in the Quick Jump list would. For the rest of the app's keys and the reasons behind them, see [Working fast: the shortcuts and why they exist](/learn/working_fast_in_tutilis).
