On a well-funded plan, **[[term:guardrails|Guardrails]]** can look almost too kind: every study stays [[term:solvent]], **extra
needed** is \$0, and the coverage strip stays green. That is honest math for a comfortable nest egg —
and a weak place to *feel* what the tool is for.

This lesson uses a **near-retire Springfield** household where the story is the opposite: the
**coverage buffer fails** under the shared sandbox, the **min portfolio for coverage** table asks
for more capital, and Forecaster **[[term:monte-carlo|Monte Carlo]]** success sits in an uncomfortable range. You then
**fatten** a holding toward the study’s solve and watch both pictures improve — without pretending
that Guardrails and Monte Carlo answer the same question.

**Prerequisite:** if Guardrails itself is new, read [Guardrail For Me](/learn/guardrail_for_me)
first (controls, onset at retirement, buffer vs Monte Carlo) — this page assumes those ideas.

The live plan on the right is **Springfield Guardrail Gap** (`springfield_guardrail_gap`) — near
retire Springfield with a **slightly leaner** investable nest egg so the study tools have to work.

This lesson carries **two plans**, because "leaner" is a claim about something. The other is
**Springfield Baseline**, the teaching root this household is derived from — same cast, same house,
same lifestyle, further from retirement and better funded. Use the plan chips above, or the links
in the prose, to put either one on the right **without leaving this page**:

> **[Look at the root →](#canvas-dossier:springfield_baseline)** to see what a comfortable version
> of this household looks like, then **[← back to the gap](#canvas-dossier:springfield_guardrail_gap)**
> to do the work. On the root, Guardrails' **extra needed** is often **\$0** and every study stays
> solvent — which is exactly the "almost too kind" picture this lesson opens by warning about.

## The question

When a published-style withdrawal study says my **starting nest egg is short** of what lifestyle funding
needs under the sandbox path, does **adding capital** (as **Min portfolio** / **Extra needed** on
Rule summary suggest) also improve residual **plan survival** under Monte Carlo — and how should I
read those two instruments side by side?

## Two instruments

Guardrails and Forecaster answer different questions, so keep their two instruments separate.

| | **Coverage buffer + min portfolio** (Guardrails) | **Monte Carlo success** (Forecaster) |
|---|---|---|
| **Question** | If draws after retirement are limited to this **study’s** allowable spend, does plan income + that capacity fund **modeled lifestyle** year by year under one sandbox path? | Under the **full plan** — residual sells, taxes, many market paths — does the path “make it” under your success definition? |
| **Who may sell the portfolio?** | Only what the **rule** allows (and, with **withdraw to need**, only the gap after other income) | Residual policy can sell what the household still needs |
| **Markets** | One constant [[term:mu\|μ]] / Set-B drag path | Cloud of sequences |

So:

- **Red buffer / positive extra needed** ≈ “this rule’s spend never fully funds lifestyle under
  these knobs.”
- **Low Silver success** ≈ “many residual-plan paths struggle under taxes and markets.”
- **Fattening the nest egg** can improve **both** — but the dollar that turns the buffer green is **not**
  guaranteed to be the dollar that hits a target success percent. Read them as **related, not
  identical.**

## Where to look

The walkthrough takes four steps. You open Guardrails, read the min portfolio while the buffer is red, grow the nest egg toward it, and then, if you like, stress the result.

### 1. Open Guardrails on this household

1. On the left **nav rail**, choose **Guardrails**.
2. In the **top section**, leave **Liquid NW** **on** so the portfolio control starts from
   **investable accounts** (not the house).
3. Keep default sandbox knobs for a first pass (**Return μ** ~**8%**, [[term:inflation]] ~**2.5%**, **Vol [[term:sigma|σ]]**
   ~**15%**) unless you are deliberately stress-testing.

You should see a **smaller** nest egg than on Springfield Baseline, and **primary retirement
only a few years out** — so pre-onset “grow only” years are short.

### 2. Read min portfolio on Rule summary while the buffer is red

Open **Rule summary** in the top band (beside Shared scenario). The last two columns — **Min
portfolio** and **Extra needed** — are the coverage solve for each rule (smallest starting nest egg
so the purchase-power buffer stays non-negative under these knobs; extra vs the portfolio control
above).

[[jump:guardrails|output__guardrails__guardrails.rule-summary__line.guardrails.min-portfolio,output__guardrails__guardrails.rule-summary__line.guardrails.extra-needed]]

Under defaults you will typically see **positive extra needed** for several rules at the current
nest egg. Magnitudes differ by rule:

| Pattern you may notice | Why it matters for this lesson |
|---|---|
| **Maggiulli** (and often **Safety-first**) ask for a **larger** starting nest egg | Percent-of-portfolio / safety-first shapes can need more capital to keep the funding buffer non-negative on this horizon |
| **Kitces** / **Guyton–Klinger** may show a **smaller** extra | Still red at this nest egg — but the solve may not lift Monte Carlo as far when you fatten only that far |
| **Extra needed = \$0** on every row | You are not on the gap story — check that this dossier is open and worths are excluded |

Pick **one** study for the walkthrough — **Maggiulli 3.65%** is a good first choice on this sample:
the extra is material, and growing the nest egg toward that min tends to move residual success into
a more comfortable band.

In the **bottom personalization** band, select that study with the chips. Leave **Withdraw to need**
on. Confirm the **coverage buffer** shows shortfalls (red) somewhere on the path — that is the
lifestyle-funding story under study-limited draws.

[[jump:guardrails|output__guardrails__guardrails.personalization__line.guardrails.coverage-buffer]]

Optional: cycle **Personalization of the Study** to **Modeled with study income** and watch the net
line after retirement onset.

### 3. Grow the nest egg toward a study min portfolio

Guardrails has already solved, for each study, the **smallest starting portfolio** that keeps the
coverage buffer from going short-funded — that is **Min portfolio** on **Rule summary** (and **Extra
needed** is how far today’s investable nest egg sits below that figure). That min is the study’s
stake in the ground for “enough nest egg to fund lifestyle under these knobs.”

Without that capital, the residual plan has a **real chance of running short**. On this sample,
Monte Carlo under Silver often lands near the **mid-40s percent** range — uncomfortable; you would
not want to retire on this nest egg as-is. Do **not** expect that percentage to equal a Guardrails
rate: the study diagnoses short funding under published-style spend; Monte Carlo answers residual
plan survival under taxes and markets.

1. Note **Min portfolio** (or **extra needed**) for the study you chose — often on the order of
   **~\$2.7M** for Maggiulli on this sample (your run may differ slightly).
2. Optionally open [[nav:birds-eye]] and turn **Liquid net worth** **on** so the headline NW excludes
   the house — same footing as Guardrails with **Exclude worths / valuables** on.
3. Open [[nav:transforms]]. In **Modifiables**, select **Traditional [[term:401k]] → Target Date Equity**<!-- term-skip: traditional — a Transform's name, not prose --> and
   use the value slider to grow the holding so **liquid** nest egg approaches that min portfolio
   (for example from ~\$707k toward ~\$1.90M — about **\$1.20M** more, or whatever your min row
   implies). Leave the house out of the target total.

[[jump:transforms]]

Transforms keeps the **original** plan beside the **modified** one. In **Simulation Summary**, run
(or re-run) Monte Carlo and read the two side by side. What you are looking for is a **large,
unmistakable lift** — the modified side should move from *uncomfortable* to *comfortable*, not
inch upward.

Do not expect to match a particular pair of numbers. How far it lifts depends on how much capital
you added, which study you solved toward, and your path count; the picture below is one run, and
yours will differ. **The lift is the claim; the score is not.** The study said the nest egg was
short, and adding capital toward Guardrails' min portfolio — not the same math as Monte Carlo —
clearly improves residual plan survival.

[[img:guardrail-gap-transforms-mc-before-after]]

*One run on this sample: **Traditional 401(k) → Target Date Equity** grown toward the Maggiulli
solve, read as Net Worth Distribution before and after. Both halves are **generated from the plans
themselves**, so they move when the engine does — but your own numbers will still differ with path
count and how much you added. The story is the **lift**, not a fixed score.*

*The **Original** and **Modified** pills are the same ones Transforms puts on the two sides in the
app, so the picture and the panel you are working in label themselves the same way.*

*Both sides share one money axis, spanning the union of the two runs, so **width means the same
thing on each** — the modified cloud reaching further right is a real difference, not a difference
in scale. Transforms does the same in the app when both columns are live, which is why the two
pictures agree. The success share is still printed on each side: the shape shows the spread, the
heading gives the score.*

If the modified side barely moves, you may have grown a non-liquid item, left **Liquid net worth**
off in [[nav:birds-eye]] (house still in the total), or targeted a study whose min is still thin relative
to residual needs — try Maggiulli’s larger solve, or add a bit more capital and compare.

### 4. Optional stress

- Lower **Return μ** toward **7%** and watch min portfolio and buffer worsen.
- Switch the personalization study among rules: notice how **extra needed** ranks differ even when
  every rule fails at the same nest egg.
- Put the root on the right — **[Springfield Baseline →](#canvas-dossier:springfield_baseline)** —
  and run Guardrails there: extras are often **\$0** under the same knobs, because the seed is not
  short. Then **[← back to the gap](#canvas-dossier:springfield_guardrail_gap)**. Seeing both under
  one set of knobs is the point; the gap sample is the foil, not a different tool.

6. One sentence: what Guardrails diagnosed vs what Monte Carlo showed after the grow.

## What this is not

The gap sample teaches a loop between two instruments, and it makes none of the claims below.

- **Not** a forecast that follows a study. Forecaster never applies a withdrawal rule; adding capital
  moves Monte Carlo success through the plan's own cash flows and residual policy.
- **Not** a claim that Maggiulli (or any study) is the “right” spend rate for you.
- **Not** identity between min \(P_0\) and the capital that hits a target Monte Carlo percent.
- **Not** advice to add exactly \$X to a 401(k) in real life — the sample is educational.
- **Not** a replacement for [[term:sequence-of-returns|sequence risk]], taxes, or residual policy experiments elsewhere in the
  app.

## Honest takeaway

Use this household to practice the loop: **Guardrails fails honestly → read min portfolio / extra
needed → grow capital in Transforms → read Original vs Modified Monte Carlo side by side.** When the
study says the nest egg is short, growing toward that min often improves residual plan survival too —
**check both instruments**, keep the questions separate, and bring your own judgment to sequence
risk and taxes.
