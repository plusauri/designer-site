# Where to retire on $20 million

Lists of the best states to retire in usually rank states by one number, most often the income tax rate. A state's taxes are not one number. An income tax grows with what you earn, a [[term:property-tax|property tax]] with the house, a sales tax with what you spend, and an [[term:estate-tax|estate tax]] with what you leave behind. Two households in the same town can pay those four taxes in very different proportions, so the same move can be a good idea for one of them and a costly one for the other. This lesson runs a $27 million couple and their $1.5 million neighbors through Region and finds that the state ranking flips between them.

## Meet the two couples

[The Vances](#canvas-dossier:vance_massachusetts) are the couple from [When bracket-filling stops working](/learn/hnw_when_bracket_filling_stops), now with their home state set. Helen is 66 and Arturo is 64, they live in Massachusetts in a $3.5 million house, and they have about $27 million in all, most of it in a taxable [[term:brokerage|brokerage]] account that holds stock index funds and $10 million of [[term:treasury-security|Treasuries]]. They spend about $420,000 a year.

[The neighbors](#canvas-dossier:neighbors_massachusetts), Joan and Ray, are the same ages and live in the same town in an $850,000 house. They have about $1.5 million, most of it in a [[term:traditional|traditional]] [[term:ira|IRA]], they spend about $92,000 a year, and [[term:ss|Social Security]] pays a large share of it.

Both couples are thinking about Florida, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. Each plan leaves its estate to two children.

## Four taxes, four bases

Each state tax is charged on something different, and that is why one ranking cannot fit everyone.

| Tax | Charged on | Who it hits hardest |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax | What comes in each year: wages, IRA withdrawals, interest, dividends, gains | Households with large taxable income. Many states exempt Social Security and some exempt retirement-plan withdrawals, but few exempt a portfolio's income. |
| Property tax | The value of the house, every year | Households whose house is a large share of what they own. |
| Sales tax | What is bought | Households that spend a large share of their income. |
| Estate or inheritance tax | What is left at death | Households with large estates. Most state estate taxes start far below the federal exemption. |

A couple whose wealth is mostly a house and an IRA meets the first three. A couple whose wealth is mostly an investment portfolio meets the first and the last, and the last can be the largest by far.

## Income tax on a portfolio

Open the tax statements for 2027 on [the Vances](#canvas-dossier:vance_massachusetts) and scroll to the Massachusetts section.

[[img:hnw-state-vance-statement-2027]]

*The Vances' 2027 tax statements. Massachusetts starts from about $815,000 of income, subtracts about $474,000 of Treasury interest, and taxes the rest at 5%, about $16,600.*

Two things stand out. Their dividends and capital gains are taxed in full, at Massachusetts's flat 5%. Their Treasury interest is not taxed at all, because federal law bars states from taxing interest on federal bonds, so the state return subtracts it. A household whose bonds are Treasuries pays far less state income tax than its income suggests. Massachusetts also adds a 4% [[term:surtax|surtax]] on income above about $1 million, which the Vances stay under because so much of their income is Treasury interest.

## The house and the shopping

Property tax follows the house wherever the household goes. Region's comparison table shows each state's average property tax rate beside its income tax. A move from Massachusetts, at about 1.0%, to New Hampshire, at about 1.6%, raises the property tax on the same house by more than half. [Property tax, the silent killer](/learn/property_tax_the_silent_killer) follows that bill through a whole retirement.

[[jump:property-tax|output__region__region.comparison__line.region.property-tax]]

[[term:sales-tax|Sales tax]] is the fourth tax. Tutilis charges it on living expenses: the state rate plus the average local rate, on the fifth of spending a sales tax typically reaches, and on groceries too where the state taxes them. It appears on the tax statement as its own card and in the cash flow as its own line, and Region's comparison counts it. Region's state profile card shows each state's rate and whether groceries are exempt, and the map's **Sales** lens shades the states that levy one. Washington's combined rate is about 9.4%, New Hampshire has none, and Massachusetts, Florida and Pennsylvania sit between 6% and 7%. Sales tax falls on what is bought, so it weighs more on a household that spends most of its income than on one that spends a small share of it. For both couples here it is real money each year, and small beside the other taxes in this lesson.

## Turn on the Estate lens

Open **Region** on [the Vances](#canvas-dossier:vance_massachusetts) and select Florida, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. Switch off the cost-of-living and wage adjustments, so each row differs from Massachusetts only in its taxes.

[[img:hnw-state-vance-region]]

*Region's comparison for the Vances with the adjustments off. Before any estate tax, Florida finishes about $2.4 million ahead of Massachusetts and Washington about $2.2 million ahead. New Hampshire finishes about $720,000 ahead and Pennsylvania about $330,000 ahead.*

Read this way, Washington looks almost as good as Florida. It has no income tax on the Vances' income, since its only income tax is on capital gains above a high threshold, and its property tax is lower than Massachusetts's. Now turn on the **Estate** lens. The comparison gains a column, **To heirs**: each row's ending [[term:net-worth|net worth]] after the federal estate tax and that state's own estate or [[term:inheritance-tax|inheritance tax]].

[[jump:estate-tax]]

[[img:hnw-state-vance-region-estate]]

*The same comparison with the Estate lens on. Florida leaves the heirs about $50.8 million, New Hampshire about $49.8 million, Pennsylvania about $47.3 million and Massachusetts about $43.4 million. Washington, second before the estate tax, falls to last at about $37.5 million.*

The ranking changes completely.

- **Florida and New Hampshire** have no estate tax. The heirs lose only the federal tax, which applies above an exemption the couple shares through [[term:portability|portability]].
- **Pennsylvania** has no estate tax, but it taxes what the children inherit at 4.5%.
- **Massachusetts** taxes estates above $2 million, about a fifteenth of the federal exemption, and the Vances lose about $7 million more to the heirs than they would in Florida.
- **Washington** has the highest state estate tax rate in the country, up to 35%. The state with no income tax on their income costs their heirs about $13 million more than Florida does.

[[img:hnw-state-map-washington]]

*Region's map with the Estate lens on: the states that levy an estate or inheritance tax are shaded, and Washington's profile card shows an estate tax with a top rate of 35%.*

A state's headline income tax can hide its largest tax. For a household at this level, the state's estate tax is often the deciding number, and it is the number least likely to appear in a ranking of places to retire.

## The neighbors' ranking

Run the same comparison on [the neighbors](#canvas-dossier:neighbors_massachusetts), with the Estate lens on.

[[img:hnw-state-neighbors-region-estate]]

*Region's comparison for the neighbors with the Estate lens on. Florida finishes about $414,000 ahead of Massachusetts and Washington about $340,000 ahead. Pennsylvania finishes slightly behind and New Hampshire about $102,000 behind. The To heirs column matches net worth in every row but Pennsylvania's.*

The neighbors' estate is far below every estate tax exemption, so the **To heirs** column changes only one row. Pennsylvania's inheritance tax has no exemption, and it charges their children 4.5% of everything they inherit, about $68,000. That takes Pennsylvania from slightly behind Massachusetts to about $96,000 behind it, level with New Hampshire. Their ranking is set by property tax and the small income tax. Washington, the worst state for the Vances, is the second best for the neighbors. New Hampshire, a good choice for the Vances, is the worst for the neighbors, because its high property tax on a house that is half of what they own outweighs having no income tax.

Same town, same four states, and the order is nearly reversed. That is the reason to run your own plan through Region rather than read a ranking.

## In-state munis

A household in a high-tax state often buys [[term:municipal-bond|municipal bonds]] issued in its own state, because most states exempt their own issuers' interest from state tax as well as federal. That doubles the tax benefit, and it also concentrates the household's bonds in one state's finances. If that state, or the projects its bonds fund, runs into trouble, the household's bond sleeve and its home state are hit together. That is [[term:credit-risk|credit risk]] with no [[term:diversification|diversification]] behind it. [When bracket-filling stops working](/learn/hnw_when_bracket_filling_stops) covers credit risk in more detail.

Tutilis tells them apart by the fund's **issuing state**, set on its yields beside the tax-exempt yield. Interest from the state the plan lives in stays off the state return, and interest from any other state is added back where the state taxes it. A national fund has no issuing state and is treated as out-of-state everywhere. Because Region swaps the state a plan lives in, it also shows the cost of a move for a household with in-state bonds: the same fund that was exempt at home is added back in the new state.

## What a move takes

A new address does not change a household's tax home. States tax residents by **domicile**, the place a person intends as their permanent home, and a high-tax state may audit a former resident who claims to have left. The evidence is ordinary life: where the household spends most of its days, where its home, cars, doctors and voter registration are, and where it files. A move made for tax reasons at this level of wealth is usually planned with an estate attorney and a tax professional, and it often comes with changes to wills and trusts.

## What the plan leaves out

- **Sales tax uses an average spending mix.** It is charged on a fixed share of living expenses, not on what this household actually buys, so a household that spends mostly on services pays less than shown and one that buys mostly goods pays more.
- **Pennsylvania's rate for young children is not modeled.** Pennsylvania charges nothing when a child 21 or younger inherits from a parent; the model taxes all children at 4.5%.
- **A move is modeled from the first year.** Region compares living in each state for the whole plan. A move partway through retirement is a second plan with the state changed from that year.

## What to take away

A state's taxes are four different taxes on four different things, and households meet them in different proportions. For most retirees, property tax and income tax set the ranking. For a household with a large estate, a state's estate or inheritance tax can outweigh everything else, and it can turn a state with no income tax into the most expensive place to die. Run your own plan through Region with the Estate lens on before believing anyone's list.
