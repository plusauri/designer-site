Brokers, fund names, and 1099s call municipal-bond interest tax-exempt. That label is a federal AGIAdjusted Gross Income (bereinigtes Bruttoeinkommen) — Gesamteinkommen abzüglich bestimmter Anpassungen und die Zahl, auf der viele andere Steuerregeln aufbauen. Es ist ein Zwischenwert, nicht Ihr zu versteuerndes Einkommen: Abzüge werden danach noch abgezogen.Full entry → fact: the interest does not go on Form 1040 as ordinary income. It is not a complete tax picture.

The same dollars still count in modified AGIModified Adjusted Gross Income (modifiziertes bereinigtes Bruttoeinkommen) — das AGI mit bestimmten wieder hinzugerechneten Abzügen. Es ist weit wichtiger, als die kleine Anpassung vermuten lässt, denn das MAGI ist die Größe, die Schwellen auslöst: Medicare-Zuschläge, die Zusatzsteuer auf Kapitalerträge und der Anspruch auf Krankenversicherungszuschüsse orientieren sich alle an einem MAGI, und jede Regel verwendet eine leicht andere Variante.Full entry → — the figure that drives IRMAAIncome-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount — ein Zuschlag auf Medicare-Beiträge für Haushalte mit höherem Einkommen. Er wirkt in Stufen, sodass ein einziger Dollar zusätzliches Einkommen den Beitrag für ein ganzes Jahr erhöhen kann. Er stützt sich auf eine Steuererklärung von vor etwa zwei Jahren, weshalb eine einmalige Spitze (eine große Umwandlung, ein Immobilienverkauf) erst viel später als Rechnung auftaucht.Full entry → Medicare premiums, the 3.8% net investment income taxNet Investment Income Tax — eine zusätzliche Steuer (üblicherweise mit 3,8 % angegeben) auf Kapitalerträge, sobald das Einkommen eine Schwelle überschreitet. Sie fällt zusätzlich zur gewöhnlichen Einkommensteuer an, nicht an ihrer Stelle, weshalb ein Plan denselben Dollar in mehr als einer Zeile besteuert zeigen kann.Full entry →, and how much of a Social Security check is taxable. And most states that levy an income tax add out-of-state municipal interest back onto their own return. California, used on these live plans, is one of them.

US Treasuries are the inverse special case: federally taxable, state-exempt, because federal law bars states from taxing that interest.

On a taxable bond sleeve of the same size, can I open the tax statement and see:

  1. National muni — federal MAGI above AGI, and a California line Municipal bond interest added back?
  2. Treasury — MAGI equals AGI, federal still taxes the interest, and California subtracts it?
  3. Corporate — neither special line; interest is ordinary at both levels?

“Tax-exempt” on a muni is answering one question: does this interest enter federal AGI? For a normal municipal bond, no.

Three other questions still have answers, and they are not “no”:

QuestionMunicipal interestTreasury interestCorporate interest
On federal AGI?NoYesYes
In MAGI (IRMAA, NIIT, taxable Social Security)?Yes — added to AGIYes (already in AGI)Yes
On a typical state return?Usually yes if the issuer is another state; usually no if it is yoursNo (federal law)Yes

A California-only muni fund would keep the in-state slice off the California return. A national fund is mostly other states. These live plans do not split issuers CUSIP by CUSIP, so they treat municipal interest the way a national fund works: added back in California. That is the conservative reading for a mixed or unknown issuer mix — it will overstate California tax on a pure in-state fund.

Illinois, Iowa, and Wisconsin tax their own munis as well. North Dakota does not add other states’ munis back. States with no income tax (Texas, Florida, …) only care about the MAGI half.

On the dossier, open the holding and the fund or stock yields section. Two fields do the work:

  • Tax-exempt interest — municipal yield. Federally excluded; California adds it back.
  • Interest yield plus US government obligation % — taxable interest, of which that percentage is Treasuries (subtracted on the state return). Treasury and TIPS growth categories default to 100%. A total-bond fund is some other percentage; you set it.

The Treasury Fund editor on Treasury, with yields open. Interest yield is 3%, and US government obligation % is 100% because the growth category is intermediate Treasury.

The National Muni Fund editor on National muni. Tax-exempt interest is 3%, and the taxable interest fields are empty.

The National muni plan has a 3% tax-exempt yield and no taxable interest. Treasury has a 3% taxable yield on an intermediate-Treasury growth category. Corporate has the same 3% taxable yield on a corporate-bond category, so none of it is subtracted.

Corporate (the control)

Live plan: Corporate

Ordinary taxable interest: in federal AGI, in MAGI, and in the California base. No add-back, no subtraction. This is what a corporate bond fund — and the taxable slice of a total-bond fund — looks like.

Corporate sleeve: neither special line. Interest is ordinary at both levels.

Municipal interest’s federal exclusion is account-sensitive. In a taxable brokerage it does what this lesson shows. Inside a traditional IRA or 401(k) the exclusion never happens: withdrawals are ordinary income regardless of what the fund held. Putting munis in tax-deferred accounts throws away the one exclusion the industry name is pointing at.

Treasuries in taxable get the state subtraction. In a 401(k) that subtraction never comes up — the state is taxing the withdrawal, not the coupon.

The live plans keep the sleeve in taxable on purpose. For the account-type contrast see Sheltering money.

The same municipal dollars that skip Form 1040 still feed:

  • IRMAA (Medicare premium steps, from MAGI two years prior)
  • NIIT (3.8% on net investment income above a MAGI threshold)
  • Taxable Social Security (provisional income includes tax-exempt interest)
