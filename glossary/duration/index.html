duration

Financial

How much a bond's or bond fund's price moves when interest rates change, measured in years. A fund with a duration of 5 loses about 5% of its price when rates rise by one percentage point, and gains about 5% when they fall by one. A long-term Treasury fund has a duration around 14 or 15, so the same one-point rise costs it about 15%. A money-market fund's duration is close to zero, so its price barely moves and its yield simply follows rates. Duration is also roughly how long it takes the higher yield to earn back the price loss after a rise, because the fund reinvests at the new rate. A bond held to maturity has a duration too, but its owner collects the face value on the maturity date whatever rates did, so the price change never has to be realized.
