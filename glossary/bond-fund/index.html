bond fund

Financial

A mutual fund or ETF that holds many bonds — hundreds or thousands — and rolls them: as bonds mature the proceeds buy new ones, so the fund itself never matures and never returns your principal on a date. That is the planning difference from owning a bond outright. A single bond held to maturity pays a contractual coupon and returns its face value on a known date, whatever rates did in between; a bond fund's price moves with rates every day and its distributions drift as its holdings turn over. One gives you a date and a promise, the other gives you diversification and liquidity without a date. Tutilis models the two differently, and the choice is yours to make. A bond fund is a *fund*: its yield is a split of its category's total return, paid out and taxed by character, with the rest as price growth. An individual bond's coupon is additive — paid by the issuer on top of the position — so the bond's own price path is flat and its category is not consulted. A bond fund entered as an individual bond therefore earns its coupon and nothing else, and quietly understates itself; the plan's Issues panel flags a bond with no maturity for exactly this reason.
