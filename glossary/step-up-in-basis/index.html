step-up in basis

Financial

At death, the cost basis of investments held in a taxable account, and of real estate, resets to their market value on the date of death. The gain built up over the owner's life is never taxed as income: an heir who sells right away owes little or no capital-gains tax, and later gains are measured from the new basis. A traditional IRA or 401(k) gets no step-up. Every dollar in it is still untaxed income, and the heir pays ordinary income tax on it as it comes out, usually within ten years. A Roth account needs no step-up, because qualified withdrawals are tax-free. This turns a common intuition around for a large estate. The taxable brokerage account, with its yearly dividends and distributions, passes to heirs with its gains forgiven, while the traditional IRA passes with its whole income tax still owed. The step-up is separate from the estate tax: an estate can owe estate tax and still pass a stepped-up basis.
