The Learning Center is meant to be useful to everyone who visits it, whether or not Tutilis ever becomes part of how you plan. The Glossary and the Reference are a large part of that. Every planner eventually knows the name of what they want without knowing where it lives. You may know you want the ten-year ruleThe SECURE Act rule for most people who inherit a retirement account after 2019: the account must be empty by December 31 of the tenth year after the owner's death. When the owner had already started RMDs, the heir must also take a distribution every year along the way. An estate gets a 5-year version when the owner had not started RMDs.Full entry →, a Roth conversionDeliberately moving money from a traditional account into a Roth one and paying the income tax on it now, so it grows and comes out tax-free later. The usual reason is a low-income year — between retiring and claiming benefits, say — where the conversion is taxed at a lower rate than the future withdrawal would be. It also shrinks later RMDs.Full entry → or IRMAAIncome-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount — a surcharge on Medicare premiums for higher-income households. It works in tiers, so one dollar of extra income can raise the premium for a whole year. It looks back at a tax return from a couple of years earlier, which is why a one-off spike (a big conversion, a property sale) shows up as a bill much later.Full entry →, but a plan has hundreds of inputs, what-if controls and forecast views, and the one that answers your question can be several clicks deep. Quick Jump is the search box at the top of the window, and it finds places rather than documents. Press Ctrl-. from anywhere, including while you are typing in a field, and the caret moves into the box. Type a word such as roth, irmaa or 529, and the box lists the places in the app where that word is entered, tried as a what-if, or shown in the forecast.

The picture shows the list for crash. Two words match, and each lists a what-if row in Transforms and the Realized returns view of the Balance Sheet chart, so a market crash can be tried on the plan and then seen in the forecast.

The list keeps those three kinds of place in that order under each word. A place where you enter a value comes first, then a row in TransformsA main-rail area. The name is Transforms because you transform a copy of the plan — a what-if — without rewriting the original dossier. Compare original vs modified side by side. where you can try it as a what-if without changing the plan, and then the tables and charts where it shows up in the forecast. Use the arrow keys to pick a place and Enter to go there, or click it. The last row under every word opens that word in the Glossary, because a word you want to find is often a word you also want explained.

Quick Jump remembers the word after you jump. A word often lives in several places, so the next one is Ctrl-. and the down arrow away, with no retyping. Press Esc to close the list, and Esc again to clear the box.

The second is the Why column of the Moves TableA Forecast Detail chip. Mov = Movements. Holding-level trades for the selected year — sold or invested, after, %, and Why., where each required withdrawal is marked as an RMDRequired Minimum Distribution — once you reach an age set by statute, the law makes you withdraw a minimum amount each year from traditional retirement accounts, whether or not you need the money. The withdrawal is taxable income, so RMDs can push a household into a higher bracket or over a surcharge threshold. Roth accounts are generally not subject to them.Full entry →. Click the scale icon on one of those rows and a small card shows the rule that applies, the deadline year, the prior balance and the amount the year requires. The net line, drawn in thick black, is net worth — often the thing you came for. Click a year to load the tables., which is where the answer to the question is easiest to see. A Roth conversion moves money from a traditional account into a RothA retirement account where you pay income tax on the money going in, and qualified withdrawals later come out tax-free — the mirror image of traditional. Named after the senator behind the law, so the word tells you nothing on its own. Plans care because Roth dollars do not add to taxable income later, which keeps them clear of income-driven cliffs.Full entry → account and pays the tax now. It is a plan assumption rather than an account, so its setting and its results live in different parts of the app. Type roth into Quick Jump and the word Roth conversion lists them together.

The Glossary explains what the words in a plan mean, from AGIAdjusted Gross Income — total income minus a specific set of adjustments, and the number many other tax rules build on. It is a midpoint, not your taxable income: deductions still come off after it.Full entry → to the ten-year rule. Press Ctrl-G to open it. The key works while you are typing in a field, because the moment you most want a definition is when the caret sits in the field that used the word, and closing the field to look it up would lose your place.

Each Glossary entry ends with a block called Where it is in the app. It lists the same places Quick Jump lists, drawn the same way, and each one is a click that takes you there. Quick Jump is that block moved to the top of the window, where people look first. The Glossary is where you go when you want the meaning as well as the place.

The Glossary is also on this site at /glossary, free for anyone to read, whether or not they use the app.

The Reference lists every table, rate and rule the forecast runs on, such as the tax brackets, the contribution limits, the IRMAA tiers and the life-expectancy tables behind required distributions. Each one names its source and its limits. Press Ctrl-R to open it, and like the Glossary it opens while you are typing.

The Reference and the Glossary look alike on purpose, because they are the same kind of thing. The Glossary holds the vocabulary and the Reference holds the numbers. The Reference is read-only, since it records what the world publishes rather than a choice in your plan. When a forecast surprises you, the Reference is where you check which figure it used.

The Reference is on this site too, at /reference, with the same free access as the Glossary.

The canvas beside this lesson is the app itself, so Quick Jump, the Glossary and the Reference all work in it. On a Mac the keys are the same Ctrl keys as in the app. On Windows and Linux the browser already uses the Ctrl-letter keys for its own commands, so the Learning Center takes the same letters with Alt instead, such as Alt-G for the Glossary.

The Jump to cards in other lessons are Quick Jump inside the page. Each card lists a word's places, and a click takes the canvas there, exactly as a row in the Quick Jump list would. For the rest of the app's keys and the reasons behind them, see Working fast: the shortcuts and why they exist.
