Lists of the best states to retire in usually rank states by one number, most often the income tax rate. A state's taxes are not one number. An income tax grows with what you earn, a property taxA yearly tax on what real estate is worth, set by the state and the local governments where it sits. It is owed whether or not the property earns anything, and rates differ several times over from one state to the next, so it is one of the larger costs a move can change. In Tutilis a home or other real asset takes its rate from its state and locality, or from a rate you set.Full entry → with the house, a sales tax with what you spend, and an estate taxA tax on what a person owns at death, paid by the estate before heirs receive it. The federal tax applies only above a large exemption ($15 million per person in 2026) at a top rate of 40%. About a dozen states and the District of Columbia have their own estate tax, most with far lower exemptions.Full entry → with what you leave behind. Two households in the same town can pay those four taxes in very different proportions, so the same move can be a good idea for one of them and a costly one for the other. This lesson runs a $27 million couple and their $1.5 million neighbors through Region and finds that the state ranking flips between them.

The Vances are the couple from When bracket-filling stops working, now with their home state set. Helen is 66 and Arturo is 64, they live in Massachusetts in a $3.5 million house, and they have about $27 million in all, most of it in a taxable brokerageAn ordinary taxable investment account — no special retirement wrapper, no contribution limit, and no withdrawal age rules. The trade-off is that dividends and realized gains are taxed as they happen rather than deferred. Often called the taxable bucket next to traditional and Roth.Full entry → account that holds stock index funds and $10 million of TreasuriesDebt issued by the US federal government. The mirror image of a municipal bond: the interest is federally taxable, and states are barred from taxing it — so it is subtracted on a state return rather than added back.Full entry →. They spend about $420,000 a year.

The neighbors, Joan and Ray, are the same ages and live in the same town in an $850,000 house. They have about $1.5 million, most of it in a traditionalA retirement account where the tax is paid on the way out — not a description of an old-fashioned account. Money went in before income tax, grew untaxed, and is taxed as ordinary income when you take it out — traditional 401(k)s and IRAs work this way. The contrast is Roth (taxed going in, tax-free coming out) and taxable (brokerage, taxed as you go).Full entry → IRAIndividual Retirement Account — a retirement wrapper you open yourself rather than through an employer. It comes in traditional (pre-tax going in) and Roth (taxed going in) flavors, so naming the flavor matters more than the acronym does.Full entry →, they spend about $92,000 a year, and Social SecuritySocial Security — the US government retirement and disability benefit, funded by payroll tax while you work and paid as a monthly check once you claim. How much you get depends on your earnings history (your PIA) and the age you claim relative to full retirement age.Full entry → pays a large share of it.

Both couples are thinking about Florida, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. Each plan leaves its estate to two children.

Each state tax is charged on something different, and that is why one ranking cannot fit everyone.

TaxCharged onWho it hits hardest
Income taxWhat comes in each year: wages, IRA withdrawals, interest, dividends, gainsHouseholds with large taxable income. Many states exempt Social Security and some exempt retirement-plan withdrawals, but few exempt a portfolio's income.
Property taxThe value of the house, every yearHouseholds whose house is a large share of what they own.
Sales taxWhat is boughtHouseholds that spend a large share of their income.
Estate or inheritance taxWhat is left at deathHouseholds with large estates. Most state estate taxes start far below the federal exemption.

A couple whose wealth is mostly a house and an IRA meets the first three. A couple whose wealth is mostly an investment portfolio meets the first and the last, and the last can be the largest by far.

Open the tax statements for 2027 on the Vances and scroll to the Massachusetts section.

The Vances' 2027 tax statements. Massachusetts starts from about $815,000 of income, subtracts about $474,000 of Treasury interest, and taxes the rest at 5%, about $16,600.

Two things stand out. Their dividends and capital gains are taxed in full, at Massachusetts's flat 5%. Their Treasury interest is not taxed at all, because federal law bars states from taxing interest on federal bonds, so the state return subtracts it. A household whose bonds are Treasuries pays far less state income tax than its income suggests. Massachusetts also adds a 4% surtaxAn extra tax charged on top of the regular one, usually on income above a high threshold, such as Massachusetts' 4% on income over about $1 million. Unlike a Medicare premium surcharge, it is a tax rate and is computed on the return. Each one shows by name under Surtaxes on the state statement.Full entry → on income above about $1 million, which the Vances stay under because so much of their income is Treasury interest.

Sales taxA tax on what is bought, charged at the register as a share of the price. Each state sets its own rate, most localities add their own, and many states exempt groceries; five states levy no statewide sales tax. In Tutilis it is charged on living expenses at the state rate plus the average local rate, on the fifth of spending a sales tax typically reaches, plus groceries where the state taxes them. A plan can set its own share and rate under Assumptions, Taxes.Full entry → is the fourth tax. Tutilis charges it on living expenses: the state rate plus the average local rate, on the fifth of spending a sales tax typically reaches, and on groceries too where the state taxes them. It appears on the tax statement as its own card and in the cash flow as its own line, and Region's comparison counts it. Region's state profile card shows each state's rate and whether groceries are exempt, and the map's Sales lens shades the states that levy one. Washington's combined rate is about 9.4%, New Hampshire has none, and Massachusetts, Florida and Pennsylvania sit between 6% and 7%. Sales tax falls on what is bought, so it weighs more on a household that spends most of its income than on one that spends a small share of it. For both couples here it is real money each year, and small beside the other taxes in this lesson.

Open Region on the Vances and select Florida, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. Switch off the cost-of-living and wage adjustments, so each row differs from Massachusetts only in its taxes.

Region's comparison for the Vances with the adjustments off. Before any estate tax, Florida finishes about $2.4 million ahead of Massachusetts and Washington about $2.2 million ahead. New Hampshire finishes about $720,000 ahead and Pennsylvania about $330,000 ahead.

Read this way, Washington looks almost as good as Florida. It has no income tax on the Vances' income, since its only income tax is on capital gains above a high threshold, and its property tax is lower than Massachusetts's. Now turn on the Estate lens. The comparison gains a column, To heirs: each row's ending net worthEverything owned minus everything owed — the bottom line of the balance sheet. Worth remembering that it counts dollars, not spendable dollars: a traditional account and a Roth account of the same size are not the same net worth to live on, because one still owes tax.Full entry → after the federal estate tax and that state's own estate or inheritance taxA tax on what an heir receives, paid by the heir, at a rate that depends on how closely the heir was related to the person who died. There is no federal inheritance tax. A handful of states levy one, and most exempt a spouse; some also exempt children.Full entry →.

Where it is in the app estate tax

Where it shows in the forecast

  • Switch on Estate tax to shade the states that levy one.

  • Each state's ending net worth after federal and state estate or inheritance tax.

The same comparison with the Estate lens on. Florida leaves the heirs about $50.8 million, New Hampshire about $49.8 million, Pennsylvania about $47.3 million and Massachusetts about $43.4 million. Washington, second before the estate tax, falls to last at about $37.5 million.

The ranking changes completely.

  • Florida and New Hampshire have no estate tax. The heirs lose only the federal tax, which applies above an exemption the couple shares through portabilityA surviving spouse's right to use the federal estate-tax exemption the first spouse to die did not use. It must be claimed on a timely federal estate-tax return, even when no tax is due. Most states with their own estate tax do not allow it.Full entry →.
  • Pennsylvania has no estate tax, but it taxes what the children inherit at 4.5%.
  • Massachusetts taxes estates above $2 million, about a fifteenth of the federal exemption, and the Vances lose about $7 million more to the heirs than they would in Florida.
  • Washington has the highest state estate tax rate in the country, up to 35%. The state with no income tax on their income costs their heirs about $13 million more than Florida does.

Region's map with the Estate lens on: the states that levy an estate or inheritance tax are shaded, and Washington's profile card shows an estate tax with a top rate of 35%.

A state's headline income tax can hide its largest tax. For a household at this level, the state's estate tax is often the deciding number, and it is the number least likely to appear in a ranking of places to retire.

Run the same comparison on the neighbors, with the Estate lens on.

Region's comparison for the neighbors with the Estate lens on. Florida finishes about $414,000 ahead of Massachusetts and Washington about $340,000 ahead. Pennsylvania finishes slightly behind and New Hampshire about $102,000 behind. The To heirs column matches net worth in every row but Pennsylvania's.

The neighbors' estate is far below every estate tax exemption, so the To heirs column changes only one row. Pennsylvania's inheritance tax has no exemption, and it charges their children 4.5% of everything they inherit, about $68,000. That takes Pennsylvania from slightly behind Massachusetts to about $96,000 behind it, level with New Hampshire. Their ranking is set by property tax and the small income tax. Washington, the worst state for the Vances, is the second best for the neighbors. New Hampshire, a good choice for the Vances, is the worst for the neighbors, because its high property tax on a house that is half of what they own outweighs having no income tax.

Same town, same four states, and the order is nearly reversed. That is the reason to run your own plan through Region rather than read a ranking.

A household in a high-tax state often buys municipal bondsDebt issued by a state or local government. Its interest is normally excluded from federal AGI — which is what the industry label *tax-exempt* means, and all it means. The same dollars still count in MAGI, and most states add back interest from other states' issuers on their own return.Full entry → issued in its own state, because most states exempt their own issuers' interest from state tax as well as federal. That doubles the tax benefit, and it also concentrates the household's bonds in one state's finances. If that state, or the projects its bonds fund, runs into trouble, the household's bond sleeve and its home state are hit together. That is credit riskThe chance that a borrower pays late, pays less than promised, or stops paying. Every bond carries some except US Treasuries, which are treated as free of it. A municipal bond is only as sound as the state, city, or project behind it, and a higher yield is usually the price a borrower pays for more of this risk.Full entry → with no diversificationSpreading money across investments that do not all move together, so a bad year for one is not a bad year for everything. Owning many holdings is not the same thing — if they are highly correlated they behave like one large position wearing several names.Full entry → behind it. When bracket-filling stops working covers credit risk in more detail.

Tutilis tells them apart by the fund's issuing state, set on its yields beside the tax-exempt yield. Interest from the state the plan lives in stays off the state return, and interest from any other state is added back where the state taxes it. A national fund has no issuing state and is treated as out-of-state everywhere. Because Region swaps the state a plan lives in, it also shows the cost of a move for a household with in-state bonds: the same fund that was exempt at home is added back in the new state.

A new address does not change a household's tax home. States tax residents by domicile, the place a person intends as their permanent home, and a high-tax state may audit a former resident who claims to have left. The evidence is ordinary life: where the household spends most of its days, where its home, cars, doctors and voter registration are, and where it files. A move made for tax reasons at this level of wealth is usually planned with an estate attorney and a tax professional, and it often comes with changes to wills and trusts.

  • Sales tax uses an average spending mix. It is charged on a fixed share of living expenses, not on what this household actually buys, so a household that spends mostly on services pays less than shown and one that buys mostly goods pays more.
  • Pennsylvania's rate for young children is not modeled. Pennsylvania charges nothing when a child 21 or younger inherits from a parent; the model taxes all children at 4.5%.
  • A move is modeled from the first year. Region compares living in each state for the whole plan. A move partway through retirement is a second plan with the state changed from that year.

A state's taxes are four different taxes on four different things, and households meet them in different proportions. For most retirees, property tax and income tax set the ranking. For a household with a large estate, a state's estate or inheritance tax can outweigh everything else, and it can turn a state with no income tax into the most expensive place to die. Run your own plan through Region with the Estate lens on before believing anyone's list.
