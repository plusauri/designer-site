On a well-funded plan, GuardrailsSpending rules that react to how the plan is doing — raise spending when the portfolio runs ahead, trim it when it falls behind — instead of holding one number for thirty years. They trade a perfectly steady income for a much smaller chance of running out, and they are the practical answer to sequence of returns risk. Tutilis never applies guardrails in your forecast, which spends what your cash flows and residual strategy say. The Guardrails panel runs the published rules on a simple side path beside your plan, so you can compare them.Full entry → can look almost too kind: every study stays solventAble to meet obligations — in a plan, that the money did not run out before the horizon. It is a lower bar than it sounds: a path can stay solvent while spending far less than intended, which is why solvency and success are not the same test.Full entry →, extra needed is $0, and the coverage strip stays green. That is honest math for a comfortable nest egg — and a weak place to feel what the tool is for.

This lesson uses a near-retire Springfield household where the story is the opposite: the coverage buffer fails under the shared sandbox, the min portfolio for coverage table asks for more capital, and Forecaster Monte CarloThe method behind a multi-path run: draw many randomized return paths, run the plan down each one, and read the spread of what happened. Named after the casino, because the engine is repeated random sampling rather than a formula — which is why two runs of one plan differ a little, and why a success rate is a proportion of sampled futures rather than a promise.Full entry → success sits in an uncomfortable range. You then fatten a holding toward the study’s solve and watch both pictures improve — without pretending that Guardrails and Monte Carlo answer the same question.

So:

  • Red buffer / positive extra needed ≈ “this rule’s spend never fully funds lifestyle under these knobs.”
  • Low Silver success ≈ “many residual-plan paths struggle under taxes and markets.”
  • Fattening the nest egg can improve both — but the dollar that turns the buffer green is not guaranteed to be the dollar that hits a target success percent. Read them as related, not identical.

3. Grow the nest egg toward a study min portfolio

Guardrails has already solved, for each study, the smallest starting portfolio that keeps the coverage buffer from going short-funded — that is Min portfolio on Rule summary (and Extra needed is how far today’s investable nest egg sits below that figure). That min is the study’s stake in the ground for “enough nest egg to fund lifestyle under these knobs.”

Without that capital, the residual plan has a real chance of running short. On this sample, Monte Carlo under Silver often lands near the mid-40s percent range — uncomfortable; you would not want to retire on this nest egg as-is. Do not expect that percentage to equal a Guardrails rate: the study diagnoses short funding under published-style spend; Monte Carlo answers residual plan survival under taxes and markets.

The gap sample teaches a loop between two instruments, and it makes none of the claims below.

  • Not a forecast that follows a study. Forecaster never applies a withdrawal rule; adding capital moves Monte Carlo success through the plan's own cash flows and residual policy.
  • Not a claim that Maggiulli (or any study) is the “right” spend rate for you.
  • Not identity between min \(P_0\) and the capital that hits a target Monte Carlo percent.
  • Not advice to add exactly $X to a 401(k) in real life — the sample is educational.
  • Not a replacement for sequence riskThe order the good and bad years arrive in. Two plans with identical average returns can end in very different places if one takes its losses early while money is being withdrawn — selling into a fall removes shares that are then not there for the recovery. It is why retirement risk is not summarized by an average, and why the years either side of the day you stop working carry more weight than their count suggests.Full entry →, taxes, or residual policy experiments elsewhere in the app.
