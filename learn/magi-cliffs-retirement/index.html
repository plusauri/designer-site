The federal statement reports AGI as a total, and MAGI builds on it as the named input that later cliff math reads.

1. AGI on the statement

The federal statement shows Adjusted gross income with non-zero ingredients (earned, pensions, traditional retirement, taxable SSSocial Security — the US government retirement and disability benefit, funded by payroll tax while you work and paid as a monthly check once you claim. How much you get depends on your earnings history (your PIA) and the age you claim relative to full retirement age.Full entry → inclusion, LTCGLong-Term Capital Gain — profit on something held longer than a year, taxed on its own lower schedule instead of at ordinary rates. The holding period is the whole trick: the same sale a day earlier is taxed as ordinary income.Full entry →, portfolio ordinary, …). That figure is the sparse worksheet AGI for the year.

2. MAGI

Modified AGI (MAGI) ≈ AGI + tax-exempt interest. When tax-exempt interest is zero (the usual case in our samples), MAGI equals AGI. The statement still labels MAGI so cliff math has a named input.

MAGI feeds:

  • NIIT (same year)
  • IRMAA (year t uses MAGI from year t−2)
  • ACA warning (same year, when pre-Medicare / marketplace healthcare is active)

IRMAA is the clearest cliff on this sample, and it arrives as a Medicare premium rather than as a tax.

Mechanism

Medicare Part B and Part D premiums step up at income brackets. The bracketA band of income taxed at a given rate. The common misreading is that entering a higher bracket taxes all your income at that rate — it does not. Only the dollars inside that band pay the higher rate, so a raise never leaves you with less after tax.Full entry → is based on MAGI from two years prior. Being one dollar over a line costs the full step for the year — for each Medicare enrollee (age 65+).

A conversion or large withdrawal at 63 can raise premiums at 65. That is the lookback.

Why not a tax line? IRMAA is a premiumWhat you pay for coverage — a monthly insurance or Medicare cost — and not a bonus or a prize. Confusingly, the same word means "above face value" when applied to a bond. In a plan it is nearly always the insurance sense: a recurring outflow that rises with age and, past a threshold, with income.Full entry →, not Form 1040 income tax. Keeping it out of totalTaxes matches how money leaves the plan: as healthcare cost, next to other medical spend.

Several other income-driven rules sit beside IRMAA. Two of them, NIIT and the Additional Medicare tax, are explicit lines on the federal statement, and the others get a short note each.

Additional Medicare tax (0.9%)

  • Applies to earned incomeMoney you were paid for working — wages, salary, self-employment profit. The distinction is not fussiness: earned income carries payroll tax and builds Social Security credit, while investment income does not. It is also what contribution limits and some credits are measured against.Full entry → above a filing-status threshold (e.g. $200k single / $250k joint).
  • Thresholds are not inflation-indexed — held nominalA face-value figure, with no adjustment for inflation. A balance that doubles in nominal terms over 25 years may buy little more than it does today. The counterpart is real, which is inflation-adjusted and closer to what the money will actually buy — always check which one a chart shows.Full entry → in the engine (same honesty choice as Social Security provisional-income thresholds).
  • Base Medicare 1.45% remains its own line; the 0.9% is additional and explicit.

NIIT (3.8%)

  • Tax = 3.8% × min(net investment income, MAGI − threshold).
  • Thresholds match the Additional Medicare statutory dollars (also not indexed).
  • Sparse NII: LTCG, dividends, interest, passive incomeIncome that arrives without work being done for it — rent, royalties, annuity-like payouts, a business you own but do not run. In a plan it is a flow that continues into retirement on its own; in tax law it is a specific category, and the two do not perfectly agree.Full entry → — not wages, not traditional IRAIndividual Retirement Account — a retirement wrapper you open yourself rather than through an employer. It comes in traditional (pre-tax going in) and Roth (taxed going in) flavors, so naming the flavor matters more than the acronym does.Full entry → distributions, not Social Security.

Did you know?

The $200,000 single and $250,000 joint thresholds for the 3.8% net investment income tax have not changed since the tax began in 2013. Congress wrote them as fixed dollar amounts with no inflationA rise in the price level. The same basket of goods costs more dollars than it did. A household plan carries a growth rate on each bill, because one published inflation number is one basket. The reporting rate that restates future dollars into today's dollars is a separate assumption.Full entry → adjustment. As prices and incomes rise, more households cross them every year without being any richer in real terms.

Early-withdrawal penalty (honesty)

The base 10% early-withdrawal additional tax on pre-59½ traditional distributions is modeled. Statutory exemptions (rule of 55, 72(t), …) are not modeled — the statement label says so. This sample is Medicare-age, so the early-penalty line should stay dark.

AMT (footnote)

Sparse AMT (AMTIAlternative Minimum Taxable Income — the income figure the AMT calculation starts from: ordinary taxable income with certain deductions added back. A plan that adds nothing back treats AMTI as roughly equal to AGI, which is why its AMT line is so often zero.Full entry → ≈ AGI, no preference add-backs) may appear when tentative minimum exceeds regular income tax. On many realistic samples it is $0. It is not the star of this lesson.

ACA

When a healthcare phase is marked as a marketplace plan, the engine prices the premium tax credit from the household's income, the poverty line and the benchmark premium, and the year's Cash Flow shows it as its own income line. The five-year runway lesson walks through one. When a marketplace-looking phase is not marked, the engine records an honesty note instead: MAGI can change premium tax credits, and this phase gets none. This sample is on Medicare, so neither applies to it.
