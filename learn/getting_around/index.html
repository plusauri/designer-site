What is in it

People — who is in the household, birth years, retirement and longevity, and roles. HoverOn a desktop or laptop, rest the pointer on a row, chip, or control to open a detail card — attributes, amounts, and often a small path chart. We put a lot of teaching detail in those cards on purpose. Hover needs a pointer, so it does not work on tablets or phones (tap may show a simpler view or nothing). Hold Alt if the tips become a nuisance — many product hovers stay closed while Alt is down so you can move the pointer freely.Full entry → a person for their modeled ages. Springfield has staggered retirement: Homer and Marge stop working in different years, visible as earned incomeMoney you were paid for working — wages, salary, self-employment profit. The distinction is not fussiness: earned income carries payroll tax and builds Social Security credit, while investment income does not. It is also what contribution limits and some credits are measured against.Full entry → spans of different lengths on the timeline.

Assets and liabilities — accounts, holdings, home, mortgages. On Springfield the house is named Evergreen Terrace so you can find it quickly.

Cash flows — the list of recurring series: salaries, lifestyle phases, Social Security, healthcare, debt service. Most are patterns ("earned income until this person retires") rather than a typed amount per year; expanding them is how the Forecaster draws multi-decade charts.

Life events — work years, retirement spans, claim markers, and one-off events, per person. The fast answer to "when does work stop, and does income line up with that?"

The tax lens

Both are pinned to 2038, a year with something to say: the brackets card shows how much ordinary income still fits under the ceiling you pick, and the radar names the thresholds actually in play that year, how far over the line the year lands, and what crossing costs.

Simulation Summary

The other half of the split answers different questions: the shape of terminal net worth, the success rate under your success definition, and the Key Forecasts (Min, quartiles, mean, Max, GMF). Click a key row and Forecast Detail focuses that path, so you can go read why it was extreme.

Read the spread, not any single row — and care about the direction a change moves the success rate more than the percentage itself.

  1. Open Modifications.
  2. Pick something in Modifiables — often a person, or a cash-flow item such as earned income.
  3. Edit a control: retirement year, active years, spend. Editing it is adding it to the Change Set; there is no separate "add" step.
  4. Charts and tables update live, so you see the plan react before any re-simulation.
  5. Re-simulate when you want the full original-versus-modified comparison.

Two habits worth learning once

Copy. The two-overlapping-pages icon on chart headers, tables, and many lists puts Markdown or image data on the clipboard. It is on generated outputs and on inputs like tax schedules and growth assumptions, on purpose — paste them into notes or a chat rather than retyping from a screenshot.

Hold Alt to quiet the tooltips. Hover explanations are everywhere, which is what you want while learning and not what you want while presenting. Holding Alt suppresses them for as long as you hold it. If the default is too chatty, the small filled/hollow circle beside the theme control reverses the rule, so tooltips appear only while Alt is down. Short hints on icon buttons and the reveal on a truncated label always show either way — an unreadable label is a dead end, not a distraction.
