While both are working, salaries support a higher lifestyle spend and room to save. After each person retires, their earned income stops. A word on “templates” (we sometimes call these generators): many cash-flow lines are not typed year-by-year. You describe a pattern — “salary until primary retirement,” “lifestyle phase from retirement to death” — and the plan expands that into yearly amounts. That is why changing one retirement age can move many series together. You do not need the implementation detail; you need the habit: look for what a line is pinned to (a calendar year vs a life event).

When you want a representative middle-of-the-road picture of the future — the one to reach for when thinking about “the” plan path — use the GMF (geometric meanThe compound growth rate that actually takes a starting balance to an ending one — the return you lived through rather than the one you were quoted. It is always at or below the arithmetic mean, equal only if every year is identical, and further below as volatility rises. This is the mean the product's north-star path is built on.Full entry → forecast), typically FID 0, our north star. Like every other single path, it is not guaranteed to happen; it is a stable, non-random representative of the middle of the cloud, not a prophecy.

Under default product settings (taxes on, sample growth as authored), Monte Carlo success for this household is meant to sit in a near-miss band: interesting for levers, not a free win and not a wipeout.

Do not treat any single percentage as scripture. The model, your success definition (Gold / Silver / Bronze), and random paths all move the number. In these lessons, prefer:

  • direction (“later retirement usually helps”), and
  • order of magnitude (“combined earnings in the low-to-mid six figures; lifestyle a step below while working”),

over memorizing a quoted rate or dollar figure.

Where you try it as a what-if

  • Move retirement and death ages to run the same plan to a different horizon.

The question

If you change when Homer (or Marge) stops working, does the whole plan respond — income, lifestyle phase, drawdown years — without you retyping every cash-flow date?

That is the star idea of this half of the lesson: life-event pins (especially retirement) plus one control that re-resolves dependent money.

Why this matters

Most tools let you edit a retirement age. The fluid feel here comes from pinning many series to named life events (for example primary retirement) instead of hard-coding the same calendar year on every line. Salary can end at retirement; the Retire living phase can start there; other ranges that use the same names move with one change.

A second product habit: TransformsA main-rail area. The name is Transforms because you transform a copy of the plan — a what-if — without rewriting the original dossier. Compare original vs modified side by side. holds a lightweight what-if over this baseline. You compare original vs modified, then Re-simulate for a full side-by-side Monte Carlo. A scenario comparison is for reading the delta — it is not a second permanent dossier unless you choose to keep it.

One thing the control does not do: it shows what a given retirement year does to the picture. It does not announce when someone is “allowed” to retire.

Try this — move retirement

  1. Stay on this Springfield sample. Note baseline Monte Carlo success and a rough sense of terminal wealth (direction and order of magnitude only).
  2. Open TransformsA main-rail area. The name is Transforms because you transform a copy of the plan — a what-if — without rewriting the original dossier. Compare original vs modified side by side. → Modifications.
  3. In Modifiables, select Forecast End (not a person row). That is where you control retirement and death dates for the household (primary and secondary), including the shared plan horizon.
  4. Move primary retirement later by a few years (the retirement control for Homer / primary under Forecast End). Changing the control adds it to the Change Set. Charts and tables in the Transforms / Forecaster workspace should update live — earned income should extend; the lifestyle Work → Retire handoff should move with it.
  5. Re-simulate for a full Monte Carlo side-by-side. Compare original vs modified:
    • Later retirement often helps success and late-life wealth (more earn years, shorter draw) — still read the charts; taxes and spend matter.
  6. Try the opposite: earlier primary retirement. Expect more stress on the portfolio if the baseline was already near-miss.
  7. Optional: under the same Forecast End editor, nudge Marge’s (secondary) retirement the other way and watch staggered dual income on the timeline.
  8. Optional: open the cash-flow chart and scrub the year axis across the old and new retirement years so the handoff is visible, not only the success percentage.

Reading the result

  • Prefer “a few years later / earlier” over a single magic retirement age.
  • Prefer “success up / down” and “terminal wealth more / less comfortable” over locking a percentage.
  • Moving only Homer’s retirement still leaves Marge’s income until her retire year — that is part of the story, not a bug.
