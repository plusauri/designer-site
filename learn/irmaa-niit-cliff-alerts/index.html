MAGIModified Adjusted Gross Income — AGI with certain deductions added back. It matters far out of proportion to the small adjustment, because MAGI is the figure that triggers cliffs: Medicare surcharges, the investment surtax, and health-subsidy eligibility all test against a MAGI, and each rule uses a slightly different one.Full entry → cliffs are easy to understand in principle and easy to lose on a busy year: IRMAAIncome-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount — a surcharge on Medicare premiums for higher-income households. It works in tiers, so one dollar of extra income can raise the premium for a whole year. It looks back at a tax return from a couple of years earlier, which is why a one-off spike (a big conversion, a property sale) shows up as a bill much later.Full entry → lives on cash flow as a healthcare surchargeAn extra amount added on top of a normal charge once income passes a line — in planning, almost always the Medicare premium surcharge. It is not a tax rate, so it does not show up in a bracket; it arrives as a higher premium, in tiers, based on a tax return from two years earlier.Full entry →, NIITNet Investment Income Tax — an extra tax (commonly cited at 3.8%) on investment income once income passes a threshold. It applies on top of ordinary income tax rather than instead of it, which is why a plan can show the same dollar taxed on more than one line.Full entry → and Additional Medicare sit on the federal tax statement, the ACAAffordable Care Act — the law behind the individual health-insurance marketplace and its income-based subsidies. It matters to a plan mostly before Medicare age: retiring early means buying coverage here, and the subsidy is tested against income you partly control.Full entry → subsidy is priced only for a plan marked as bought on the marketplace, and a Roth conversionDeliberately moving money from a traditional account into a Roth one and paying the income tax on it now, so it grows and comes out tax-free later. The usual reason is a low-income year — between retiring and claiming benefits, say — where the conversion is taxed at a lower rate than the future withdrawal would be. It also shrinks later RMDs.Full entry → can move MAGI now while IRMAA may bite two years later.

You do not need a new nav destination. The radar lives in place on the path you already use to read a year.

The whole card. Upper canvas: MAGI and lookback MAGI as solid bars — your path — against hatched threshold lines (NIIT floor, IRMAA line) that are published schedule, not your plan. That solid-vs-hatch split is the card's grammar and the legend states it. Lower canvas: cliff cash actually assessed, hanging below the axis so a cost never reads as income. Lane chips toggle each series.

This year shows the Nearby status. MAGI is still under the NIIT line of $250,000, but by less than $25,000, so the card names NIIT before the plan crosses it. Nothing is in play yet. Not modeled · ACA subsidy is still listed, because a gap we know about is not the same as a gap we hide.

Each cliff uses one of these statuses:

StatusMeans
In playThreshold already crossed for this year — modeled tax or IRMAA surcharge applies
NearbyStill under the next line, within $25,000 — explainable proximity, not a score
Not modeledKnown cliff we name honestly; we do not invent cash response
BelowThe ACA row when its credit is priced and the household is more than $25,000 under the 400% line; it shows the credit received

There are no optimum markers, cliff scores, or “best conversion” badges. The strip reports where you stand relative to lines the engine already knows, not which path is best.

IRMAA in play. Selected year stacks the whole cause chain for one year in words: the tier, the lookback calendar year, the lookback MAGI that actually decided the surcharge, how far this year sits below the next line, and what the surcharge costs. NIIT is stated the same way on its own row, against its own threshold, and ACA stays listed as not modeled.

The “below next line” figure is the part worth internalizing: it is how close this year sits to the next tier, which is a decision input long before you cross it. This is the whole promise of the card — the lookback story readable in one place, instead of reconstructed from a cash-flow table two years apart.

This sample’s income is mostly retirement distributions, so Additional Medicare may stay quiet while NIIT and IRMAA still appear — that contrast is intentional on the Banks cliff path.

ACA is always listed on a US plan. When a healthcare phase is marked as a marketplace plan, the engine prices the premium tax credit, and the row shows where the household sits against the 400% line and what the credit is: Below or Nearby with the credit received, or In play over the line with the credit that crossing it cost. The five-year runway lesson shows both sides. This sample is on Medicare, and a plan with no marked marketplace phase gets the Not modeled row: named gap, not a priced response.

When no modeled cliff is in play or nearby, the card says so calmly — for example, that nothing modeled is near this year — and still lists the ACA row, priced or not modeled, so the marketplace question is never silently omitted. Quiet is a feature; fake urgency is not.

A conversion raises same-year MAGI. IRMAA, when Medicare age has arrived, reads MAGI from two years earlier. The radar surfaces that link without forcing you to rebuild it:

SituationWhat the strip can show
Conversion active this yearNote that conversion raises same-year MAGI (and feeds IRMAA lookback two years later)
Conversion in the IRMAA lookback yearOn the IRMAA row: conversion in lookback year feeds premiums for the selected year

On this Banks sample, conversions may not be the star — use Roth conversion in the window (roth_conversion_sample) when you want policy, Money Movements Why, and the conversion year trail. That sample may not light IRMAA cash every year; the radar still must not invent a surcharge when the engine has none, and it should still mark conversion lookback when Medicare ages in.

This lesson deliberately makes no claim about the following:

  • A multi-cliff optimizer or recommended conversion amount
  • ACA premium tax creditThe ACA subsidy that pays part of a marketplace health premium, sized by income. It is the reason a dollar of extra income can cost far more than its tax: crossing the wrong line trims or ends the subsidy for the whole year.Full entry → dollars for a plan whose healthcare phase is not marked as a marketplace plan, or Medicaid
  • Estate, state, or non-MAGI cliffs on this strip
  • That Nearby is a personalized risk score (it is a fixed dollar band under the next line)
  • That every sample year will show IRMAA cash (depends on lookback MAGI, Medicare age, and path)

Those are honesty boundaries. The radar names what is in play; it does not solve the plan for you.
