Healthcare in a US plan is usually not one number for life. Costs change when employer coverage ends, when marketplace or private coverage fills a pre-Medicare gap, and when Medicare starts around age 65. This lesson shows that handoff as phases on a medical cash-flow template so the forecast does not pretend medical spend is free until someone notices.

This lesson uses the same household as Meet the Springfield household, Homer and Marge, and the plan already carries a few medical lines: one family health plan that covers both of them, and a Medicare line for each. The amounts are planning placeholders rather than quotes.

A couple crosses 65 twice, so the handoff is a chain of three phases across three templates rather than one step:

What changes at each milestone

The three templates are not a modeling flourish. They are the three policies a US couple actually buys between mid-career and old age, and each one starts and stops on a different trigger.

  • While someone is still employed, the household is usually on one family plan with one premium. The number you see on a pay stub is the employee share; the employer pays the rest, and that subsidy is the first thing that disappears at retirement.
  • Retiring before 65 opens a gap. COBRA continues the employer plan for up to 18 months, at the full premium rather than the employee share. After that, the choices are a spouse's plan or the ACAAffordable Care Act — the law behind the individual health-insurance marketplace and its income-based subsidies. It matters to a plan mostly before Medicare age: retiring early means buying coverage here, and the subsidy is tested against income you partly control.Full entry → marketplace, where the premium depends on age and on income. For a couple who retires early these are typically the most expensive healthcare years of their lives per person, and they are the years a plan most often leaves out, because the family plan "just continued" in the planner's head.
  • Medicare starts at 65, per person, not per household. Part B and Part D carry monthly premiums, a Medigap or Medicare Advantage policy sits on top, and all of it is enrolled and billed under one person's name. A couple therefore crosses 65 twice: one spouse moves to Medicare while the other stays on whatever covered them before, then the second follows. Springfield's version is Homer in 2041 and Marge in 2043, with Marge on a self-only tail of the family plan in between.
  • Income raises the Medicare bill. The IRMAA surcharge is set from MAGIModified Adjusted Gross Income — AGI with certain deductions added back. It matters far out of proportion to the small adjustment, because MAGI is the figure that triggers cliffs: Medicare surcharges, the investment surtax, and health-subsidy eligibility all test against a MAGI, and each rule uses a slightly different one.Full entry → two years back and charged per enrollee, so a spouse who is still working when the other enrolls can push the retiree's premium up for the first Medicare years. The surcharge fades on its own once the lookback sees retirement income.
  • Medical costs grow faster than general prices. Premiums, deductibles and drug costs have outrun headline inflationA rise in the price level. The same basket of goods costs more dollars than it did. A household plan carries a growth rate on each bill, because one published inflation number is one basket. The reporting rate that restates future dollars into today's dollars is a separate assumption.Full entry → for decades, which is why each phase carries its own growth rate rather than the plan-wide one. Commonly cited estimates for what a 65-year-old will spend on healthcare over retirement run into the low hundreds of thousands of dollars per person, before long-term careLong-term care — help with everyday living (in-home aide, assisted living, nursing care) rather than medical treatment for an illness. It is modeled separately because ordinary health coverage largely does not pay for it, and because it tends to arrive late in a plan as a large, multi-year expense.Full entry →.

Model one policy as one line, because that is how the bills arrive and how they end. A family plan is one premium covering two people, so it is one line that names both. Medicare is one premium per person, so it is one line per spouse with that spouse's own start year. You could squash the whole household into a single four-phase chain and the forecast would charge the same money, but "what does Homer's Medicare cost" would stop being a number in the plan, and the IRMAA surcharge would no longer sit beside the premium it surcharges.

What an HSA is, in the real world

Did you know?

A health savings account is the only account in the US tax code with a triple tax advantage. Contributions go in before tax, growth is never taxed along the way, and withdrawals for qualified medical costs come out tax free. A 401(k)An employer-sponsored retirement account, named after the section of tax law that created it — so the name says nothing about what it does. Contributions are usually payroll-deducted, often matched by the employer, and come in traditional (pre-tax) and Roth flavors. A 403(b) is the same idea for schools and nonprofits, a 457 for government workers.Full entry → gets the first two and taxes the third, and a RothA retirement account where you pay income tax on the money going in, and qualified withdrawals later come out tax-free — the mirror image of traditional. Named after the senator behind the law, so the word tells you nothing on its own. Plans care because Roth dollars do not add to taxable income later, which keeps them clear of income-driven cliffs.Full entry → gets the last two and taxes the first.

The three legs, side by side:

LegReal-world treatment
Going inContributions are deductible (or pre-tax via payroll)
While investedGrowth is not taxed year to year
Coming outWithdrawals for qualified medical are tax free

Contributions stop at 65 — and that is not a modeling shortcut

Open the contribution and look at Active years: it ends in 2041, the year Homer turns 65. HSA eligibility ends when Medicare enrolment begins — you may spend an HSA for the rest of your life, but you may not put money in once you are on Medicare.

That real rule lands on the very year this lesson's employer → Medicare handoff already turns on. The same birthday that changes what medical costs also closes the account that was funding it.

How far does it carry?

Because the account is funded during working years and drawn on for life, its balance has a shape worth reading. That is the useful planning answer, and it is not a tax number: how many years of a known, dated, lifelong expense does this account absorb before the rest of the plan has to?

Reading the numbers. Balances on the canvas move with the display settings — today's-dollars vs nominal — and with the forecast path selected. Watch the shape (rise, turn, empty) and read your own dates off the plan; a figure quoted in prose would be wrong for half the views you can put the canvas in.

What the forecast does not give the HSA

Be careful reading a benefit into the net-worth line here. This product models an HSA as a tax-deferredTax postponed, not avoided. Growth compounds without a yearly tax drag, and the bill arrives when the money comes out — which is why a large tax-deferred balance and a required withdrawal age are the same conversation.Full entry → account — the same treatment a traditionalA retirement account where the tax is paid on the way out — not a description of an old-fashioned account. Money went in before income tax, grew untaxed, and is taxed as ordinary income when you take it out — traditional 401(k)s and IRAs work this way. The contrast is Roth (taxed going in, tax-free coming out) and taxable (brokerage, taxed as you go).Full entry → 401(k) gets. Growth is not taxed year to year, but the third leg, tax-freeNo income tax due when the money comes out — the Roth promise, and the reason Roth dollars keep a withdrawal clear of income-driven thresholds. The word hides a condition: the withdrawal has to be qualified, which turns on age and how long the account has been open. It also hides a scope: municipal-bond interest is tax-free on the federal return yet still counts toward whether Social Security is taxed.Full entry → withdrawal for qualifiedA tax word meaning the conditions were met, not a judgment of quality. A qualified Roth withdrawal is tax-free; an unqualified one is not. Qualified dividends get the lower capital-gain rates; ordinary ones do not. Whenever you see it, the interesting question is *which* conditions.Full entry → medical, is not modeled, and no deductionAn amount subtracted from income before the tax is computed, so it saves tax at your marginal rate rather than dollar for dollar. That is the difference from a credit, which comes off the tax itself: a $1,000 deduction is worth a few hundred dollars, a $1,000 credit is worth $1,000.Full entry → is modeled going in either.

So the plan is conservative about an HSA: a real one is worth more than this forecast shows. Use this canvas for the mechanics — the contribution, the 65 cut-off, the funding link, the years covered — and treat the tax advantage as a real-world reason to prefer the account, not as something you can read off the chart.

That is also why the balance above is the honest headline rather than a net-worth comparison: what the account demonstrably does here is absorb an expense, and absorbing an expense is worth knowing about on its own.

B. See the coverage check catch a gap

Desktop app only
  1. Open Springfield Baseline (or any plan with early retirement).
  2. Delete the Family health plan template, leaving only the two Medicare lines, with retirement age < 65 on a person.
  3. Expect an inline warning on each Medicare template: that owner's pre-65 years have no marketplace / employer / private phase — coverage would look free until 65. The check reads every template covering a person, so a family plan that stops at 65 and a Medicare line that starts there satisfy it together.
  4. Add a pre-Medicare template (a new healthcare entry seeds US defaults ending at the primary's Medicare year; set Covers to both owners) — the warning should clear.
  5. Symmetric check: delete an owner's Medicare template while their death age > 65 — warning that the plan runs past 65 without a Medicare-era boundary for that person.
