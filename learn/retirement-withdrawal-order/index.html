In pure drawdownThe spending-down phase — living off the portfolio rather than adding to it. Watch out: in market commentary the same word means a peak-to-trough decline. In planning prose here it is the withdrawal phase of life, the counterpart to the accumulation years.Full entry →, something has to fund living spend when cash flow is short. After any linked funding rules fire, the plan’s residual capital strategy decides which accounts supply the rest — and that choice can change terminal wealthEnding net worth (or a close cousin) at the horizon of the forecast — often the flag year on Key Forecasts (Min / GMF / Max and percentiles) and the spread of endings on Net Worth Distribution. This lesson uses a lean solo retired Homer sample sized so two strategies diverge:

StrategyPlain idea
Pro-rata (this sample’s default)Sell residual need market-value pro-rata across holdings. Honest baseline; does not prefer tax buckets.
Tax-account orderDrain taxable first, then tax-deferred (e.g. traditional IRA), then tax-free (Roth / 529). Protects high-growth tax-free accounts longer.

The sample is built so that, with large taxable cash and a large high-growth RothA retirement account where you pay income tax on the money going in, and qualified withdrawals later come out tax-free — the mirror image of traditional. Named after the senator behind the law, so the word tells you nothing on its own. Plans care because Roth dollars do not add to taxable income later, which keeps them clear of income-driven cliffs.Full entry →, tax-account order should beat pro-rata on terminal net worth when you flip the strategy and re-simulate.
