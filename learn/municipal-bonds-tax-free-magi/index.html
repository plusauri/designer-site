Brokers, fund names, and 1099s call municipal-bondDebt issued by a state or local government. Its interest is normally excluded from federal AGI — which is what the industry label *tax-exempt* means, and all it means. The same dollars still count in MAGI, and most states add back interest from other states' issuers on their own return.Full entry → interest tax-exempt. That label is a federal AGIAdjusted Gross Income — total income minus a specific set of adjustments, and the number many other tax rules build on. It is a midpoint, not your taxable income: deductions still come off after it.Full entry → fact: the interest does not go on Form 1040 as ordinary incomeIncome taxed at the regular graduated rates — wages, interest, pension payments, and withdrawals from traditional retirement accounts. The point of the label is the contrast: long-term capital gains and qualified dividends get their own, usually lower, rate schedule.Full entry →. It is not a complete tax picture.

The same dollars still count in modified AGIModified Adjusted Gross Income — AGI with certain deductions added back. It matters far out of proportion to the small adjustment, because MAGI is the figure that triggers cliffs: Medicare surcharges, the investment surtax, and health-subsidy eligibility all test against a MAGI, and each rule uses a slightly different one.Full entry → — the figure that drives IRMAAIncome-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount — a surcharge on Medicare premiums for higher-income households. It works in tiers, so one dollar of extra income can raise the premium for a whole year. It looks back at a tax return from a couple of years earlier, which is why a one-off spike (a big conversion, a property sale) shows up as a bill much later.Full entry → Medicare premiums, the 3.8% net investment income taxNet Investment Income Tax — an extra tax (commonly cited at 3.8%) on investment income once income passes a threshold. It applies on top of ordinary income tax rather than instead of it, which is why a plan can show the same dollar taxed on more than one line.Full entry →, and how much of a Social Security check is taxable. And most states that levy an income tax add out-of-state municipal interest back onto their own return. California, used on these live plans, is one of them.

US TreasuriesDebt issued by the US federal government. The mirror image of a municipal bond: the interest is federally taxable, and states are barred from taxing it — so it is subtracted on a state return rather than added back.Full entry → are the inverse special case: federally taxable, state-exempt, because federal law bars states from taxing that interest.

On a taxable bond holding of the same size, can I open the tax statement and see:

  1. National muni — federal MAGI above AGI, and a California line Municipal bond interest added back?
  2. Treasury — MAGI equals AGI, federal still taxes the interest, and California subtracts it?
  3. Corporate — neither special line; interest is ordinary at both levels?

“Tax-exempt” on a muni is answering one question: does this interest enter federal AGI? For a normal municipal bond, no.

Three other questions still have answers, and they are not “no”:

QuestionMunicipal interestTreasury interestCorporate interest
On federal AGI?NoYesYes
In MAGI (IRMAA, NIIT, taxable Social Security)?Yes — added to AGIYes (already in AGI)Yes
On a typical state return?Usually yes if the issuer is another state; usually no if it is yoursNo (federal law)Yes

A California-only muni fund would keep the in-state slice off the California return. A national fund is mostly other states. These live plans do not split issuers CUSIPThe nine-character id that names one specific security. It matters here because tax character is a property of the issuer, so questions like "how much of this fund is in-state?" are answered issue by issue rather than by the fund's name.Full entry → by CUSIP, so they treat municipal interest the way a national fund works: added back in California. That is the conservative reading for a mixed or unknown issuer mix — it will overstate California tax on a pure in-state fund.

Illinois, Iowa, and Wisconsin tax their own munis as well. North Dakota does not add other states’ munis back. States with no income tax (Texas, Florida, …) only care about the MAGI half.

Municipal interest’s federal exclusion is account-sensitive. In a taxable brokerage it does what this lesson shows. Inside a traditional IRAIndividual Retirement Account — a retirement wrapper you open yourself rather than through an employer. It comes in traditional (pre-tax going in) and Roth (taxed going in) flavors, so naming the flavor matters more than the acronym does.Full entry → or 401(k) the exclusion never happens: withdrawals are ordinary income regardless of what the fund held. Putting munis in tax-deferredTax postponed, not avoided. Growth compounds without a yearly tax drag, and the bill arrives when the money comes out — which is why a large tax-deferred balance and a required withdrawal age are the same conversation.Full entry → accounts throws away the one exclusion the industry name is pointing at.

Treasuries in taxable get the state subtraction. In a 401(k) that subtraction never comes up — the state is taxing the withdrawal, not the coupon.

The live plans keep the holding in taxable on purpose. For the account-type contrast see Sheltering money.

The same municipal dollars that skip Form 1040 still feed:

  • IRMAA (Medicare premiumWhat you pay for coverage — a monthly insurance or Medicare cost — and not a bonus or a prize. Confusingly, the same word means "above face value" when applied to a bond. In a plan it is nearly always the insurance sense: a recurring outflow that rises with age and, past a threshold, with income.Full entry → steps, from MAGI two years prior)
  • NIIT (3.8% on net investment income above a MAGI threshold)
  • Taxable Social Security (provisional incomeThe test income that decides how much of a Social Security benefit is taxed: adjusted gross income, plus tax-exempt interest, plus half of the benefit itself. It is not AGI and not MAGI, and the tax-exempt line is the trap — municipal-bond interest that owes nothing on its own can still pull more of a benefit into taxable income.Full entry → includes tax-exempt interest)

Did you know?

Interest from a tax-exempt municipal bond can make more of your Social Security taxable. When the IRS works out how much of a benefit to tax, it adds that interest back in. The interest never appears as taxable incomeWhat the rates are finally applied to — income after adjustments and deductions, not what you earned. It sits at the bottom of the page: gross income, then AGI, then this. Most people's taxable income is meaningfully smaller than their pay, which is why a bracket alone never tells you the tax.Full entry → on Form 1040, yet it can still raise the tax on your benefits.

This page is the character of the interest. MAGI cliffs — IRMAA, NIIT, and friends is where those later costs show up as their own lines. On that sample MAGI usually equals AGI because there is no tax-exempt interest. Switch back here when you want MAGI to move without AGI.

  • Issuer-by-issuer in-state vs out-of-state munis. A California-only fund would keep more off the California return than a national fund.
  • Private-activity municipal bonds that can appear on the federal AMT.
