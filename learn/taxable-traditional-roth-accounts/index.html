Where you hold investments can matter as much as what you hold. The same stock market return behaves differently in a taxable brokerageAn ordinary taxable investment account — no special retirement wrapper, no contribution limit, and no withdrawal age rules. The trade-off is that dividends and realized gains are taxed as they happen rather than deferred. Often called the taxable bucket next to traditional and Roth.Full entry →, a traditional 401(k)An employer-sponsored retirement account, named after the section of tax law that created it — so the name says nothing about what it does. Contributions are usually payroll-deducted, often matched by the employer, and come in traditional (pre-tax) and Roth flavors. A 403(b) is the same idea for schools and nonprofits, a 457 for government workers.Full entry →, a RothA retirement account where you pay income tax on the money going in, and qualified withdrawals later come out tax-free — the mirror image of traditional. Named after the senator behind the law, so the word tells you nothing on its own. Plans care because Roth dollars do not add to taxable income later, which keeps them clear of income-driven cliffs.Full entry → 401(k), and a Roth IRAIndividual Retirement Account — a retirement wrapper you open yourself rather than through an employer. It comes in traditional (pre-tax going in) and Roth (taxed going in) flavors, so naming the flavor matters more than the acronym does.Full entry → — for taxes today, taxes in retirement, required distributions, and how freely you can reach the money.

This lesson names those four account types, compares the rules that actually differ, and points at the live Banks household so you can see each type on a real balance sheet. It is content-first: the forecast will not invent IRS forms for you, but the product does tax each account type according to its tax bucketWhich tax rule a dollar lives under, rather than which institution holds it: taxable (taxed as you go), traditional (taxed on the way out), Roth (taxed on the way in). Two accounts showing the same balance in different buckets are not worth the same amount to spend, which is why a plan tracks the mix and not only the total. Legal wrappers differ by country; the three buckets are the durable idea.Full entry →, force traditional RMDs when age and balance require them, and treat Roth withdrawals as federally tax-freeNo income tax due when the money comes out — the Roth promise, and the reason Roth dollars keep a withdrawal clear of income-driven thresholds. The word hides a condition: the withdrawal has to be qualified, which turns on age and how long the account has been open. It also hides a scope: municipal-bond interest is tax-free on the federal return yet still counts toward whether Social Security is taxed.Full entry → retirement income.

Cash / demand accounts in this product sit with the taxable bucket for tax treatment: no retirement shelter, no RMDRequired Minimum Distribution — once you reach an age set by statute, the law makes you withdraw a minimum amount each year from traditional retirement accounts, whether or not you need the money. The plan does not auto-enforce every IRS contribution cap — it does apply the tax bucket when money is sold or withdrawn.

College 529 plans are another taxes-paid-style vehicle aimed at education (and, in some cases, limited later paths into retirement accounts). That story is separate from these four account types.

Three sets of rules separate these accounts in practice: required minimum distributions, early withdrawals, and contribution eligibility.

Required minimum distributions (RMDs)

What they are. Once you reach RMD age and hold a traditional (tax-deferredTax postponed, not avoided. Growth compounds without a yearly tax drag, and the bill arrives when the money comes out — which is why a large tax-deferred balance and a required withdrawal age are the same conversation.Full entry →) retirement balance, the IRS expects you to take a minimum distribution each year — whether or not you need the cash for living expenses. The money is generally ordinary incomeIncome taxed at the regular graduated rates — wages, interest, pension payments, and withdrawals from traditional retirement accounts. The point of the label is the contrast: long-term capital gains and qualified dividends get their own, usually lower, rate schedule.Full entry →. Missing an RMD can trigger a large excise tax on the shortfall (this product models a default penalty rate; reduced “timely correction” rates are not the focus here).

When they start (SECUREThe law (and its sequel) that moved several retirement-account rules: the age RMDs start, catch-up contributions, and how quickly an inherited account must be emptied. Its practical effect on a plan is that dates you may remember from a decade ago are no longer the dates.Full entry → 2.0, by birth year — the engine’s approximation):

How the amount is determined. For a calendar year, a common rule of thumb is:

prior year-end balance ÷ life-expectancy divisor (Uniform Lifetime Table for many owners)

The divisor falls with age, so the required fraction of the account grows. When the force is active, this product shows the required amount each year on the Brackets chart and Money Movements Why lines such as RMD · Traditional ….

Did you know?

Until 2024, a Roth 401(k) came with required minimum distributions even though a Roth IRA never did. SECURE 2.0 removed them starting in 2024, so the two Roth accounts now follow the same rule for the original owner.

Early withdrawals (brief)

Before 59½, many traditional retirement spending distributions can face a base 10% additional tax on top of ordinary income (exceptions exist; the product models the base case). A Roth conversion is treated as a transfer, not spending — see the conversion lesson. Taxable brokerage sales are not “early withdrawal penalties”; they are capital-gains events.

A Roth conversion moves balance from traditional → Roth. You pay ordinary income tax on the converted amount now so qualifiedA tax word meaning the conditions were met, not a judgment of quality. A qualified Roth withdrawal is tax-free; an unqualified one is not. Qualified dividends get the lower capital-gain rates; ordinary ones do not. Whenever you see it, the interesting question is *which* conditions.Full entry → later growth and withdrawals can be tax-free — and so you may reduce future traditional RMDs.

That trade is most interesting in lower-income years (often after paychecks stop and before RMDs force large traditional withdrawals). Planners call that span the conversion window.

This lesson does not walk conversion policy, Money Movements Why: Roth conversion, or MAGIModified Adjusted Gross Income — AGI with certain deductions added back. It matters far out of proportion to the small adjustment, because MAGI is the figure that triggers cliffs: Medicare surcharges, the investment surtax, and health-subsidy eligibility all test against a MAGI, and each rule uses a slightly different one.Full entry → / IRMAAIncome-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount — a surcharge on Medicare premiums for higher-income households. It works in tiers, so one dollar of extra income can raise the premium for a whole year. It looks back at a tax return from a couple of years earlier, which is why a one-off spike (a big conversion, a property sale) shows up as a bill much later.Full entry → traps year by year. Use:
