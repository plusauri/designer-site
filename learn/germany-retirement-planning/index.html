Accounts to open

  1. Depot (taxable brokerageAn ordinary taxable investment account — no special retirement wrapper, no contribution limit, and no withdrawal age rules. The trade-off is that dividends and realized gains are taxed as they happen rather than deferred. Often called the taxable bucket next to traditional and Roth.Full entry →) — Europe-facing holdings: developed ex-US equity, emerging markets, Euro aggregate bonds (thin DE/EU market pack — reuses existing growth types).
  2. Girokonto (checking) — demand / cash personality.
  3. bAV (Direktversicherung sketch) — workplace pot; annual contribution until start age 67.
  4. Rürup (Basisrente sketch) — higher private contribution sketch; same contract shape for now.
  5. Riester (sketch) — own contribution plus an annual Zulagen sketch (€475 total — illustrative basic + one child, not a full statutory table by tax year).

bAV, Rürup, and Riester share a contract shell today (capital, contribution, start age, COLACost-Of-Living Adjustment — the annual raise applied to a benefit so it keeps pace with prices. Social Security has one; many pensions do not, which is the difference that decides whether a fixed payment still buys anything in twenty years.Full entry →). That is intentional MVP reuse. Labels and the sample story carry most of the identity until vehicle-specific tax and subsidy depth deepen. Riester is the one that already shows a Zulagen line expanding separately from own contribution.

A. Confirm you are not in a US plan

  1. Read the toolbar: RegionA main-rail area. Explore the plan as if you lived elsewhere (today: US states; other countries are future work). The rail label is Region. should show Germany, display unit EUR.
  2. Open accounts: you should see bAV / Rürup / Riester / Gesetzliche Rente vocabulary — not TraditionalA retirement account where the tax is paid on the way out — not a description of an old-fashioned account. Money went in before income tax, grew untaxed, and is taxed as ordinary income when you take it out — traditional 401(k)s and IRAs work this way. The contrast is Roth (taxed going in, tax-free coming out) and taxable (brokerage, taxed as you go).Full entry → IRAIndividual Retirement Account — a retirement wrapper you open yourself rather than through an employer. It comes in traditional (pre-tax going in) and Roth (taxed going in) flavors, so naming the flavor matters more than the acronym does.Full entry → / RothA retirement account where you pay income tax on the money going in, and qualified withdrawals later come out tax-free — the mirror image of traditional. Named after the senator behind the law, so the word tells you nothing on its own. Plans care because Roth dollars do not add to taxable income later, which keeps them clear of income-driven cliffs.Full entry → / Social Security as the primary cast.

You should get a Germany tax card (sketch notes included), not the US federal “totals-first” Social Security / FICAFederal Insurance Contributions Act — the payroll tax withheld from a paycheck for Social Security and Medicare. An employee pays half and the employer pays the other half. It is separate from income tax, which is why a plan can show income tax and payroll tax as different lines. C. After statutory start age

  1. Scrub Year to a year when Lukas is past 67 (sample birth 1980 → roughly plan year 2047+ depending on as-of year).
  2. On CF, look for Gesetzliche Rente as benefit cash (generator expand), distinct from taxable depot sales.
  3. On Taxes → Germany, skim ordinary vs capital tracks if the year has both wage/pension-like flows and taxable-account gains. Treat numbers as illustrative sketch, not a BMFBundesministerium der Finanzen — Germany's federal finance ministry, whose published income-tax formula and tables are the authority a German sketch is approximating.Full entry → notice.

TaxesDe is the Germany tax plugin on this sample, and it is a sketch rather than full national tax software.

The 26.375% capital rate is the 25% flat tax plus the 5.5% solidarity surchargeAn extra amount added on top of a normal charge once income passes a line — in planning, almost always the Medicare premium surcharge. It is not a tax rate, so it does not show up in a bracket; it arrives as a higher premium, in tiers, based on a tax return from two years earlier.Full entry → on that tax (25% × 1.055).

US federal benefit inclusion (0/50/85%) and FICA payroll are different systems for a different region — see Social Security treatment. Do not read the Germany card as if it were that worksheet.
