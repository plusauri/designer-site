The household financial planner that shows its work

Tutilis (too-TIL-iss) comes from tutor and utilis: a useful tutor. Work out your own retirement and financial plan, with a tutor beside you for the parts you don't know yet.

Most planning tools simplify for beginners, on purpose. Tutilis keeps the hard parts in the model and keeps them out of your way until you need them. It models what you actually own, works out a real tax return for every year it projects, and lets you trace any number back to where it came from.

Everything here ships in Tutilis today. Most items link to a lesson that shows the feature at work, so you can watch it before you take our word for it.

Accounts and investments

  • Investment holdings. You enter the funds and stocks you actually own, and each one carries its own expected return and volatility. See it: Meet the Springfield household.
  • Portfolio allocations. If you would rather not list holdings, enter an account as one balance with an asset mix. See it: Glide paths through retirement.
  • 28 account types. Traditional and Roth 401(k), 403(b), 457(b) and TSP accounts, plus Traditional and Roth IRAs, SEP, SIMPLE, profit-sharing plans, 529s, HSAs, pensions and inherited IRAs. See it: Sheltering money.
  • Dividends and interest. Each payout is reinvested or taken as cash, and each is taxed correctly: qualified dividends at capital-gains rates, and other dividends and interest as ordinary income. See it: The dividend you never see.
  • Cost basis. Selling in a taxable account results in an actual taxable gain, measured from what you paid. See it: Capital gains, dividends and interest in retirement.
  • Contributions and matches. Your contributions grow with the employer match, and the plan follows the IRS limits and catch-up rules. See it: Filling the cheap buckets.
  • Equity compensation. RSUs, NSOs and restricted stock vest on their schedules, and you choose whether to sell at vest or hold. See it: Equity compensation as it vests.
  • Net worth projection. You see today's balance sheet and your projected net worth for every year of the plan. See it: Reading the charts and tables.

Real estate and debt

  • Primary residence. Your home carries its running costs and property tax, and you can plan its sale. See it: Property tax, the silent killer.
  • Second home or vacation home. Add as many as you own, each with its own costs, taxes and sale.
  • Rentals and businesses. Rental property brings in income, and a business can be sold in any year you choose, in whole or in part. See it: Selling the business when you retire.
  • Mortgages and loans. Mortgages and loans pay down on schedule, or sooner when you set a larger payment, and selling a home pays off its mortgage. HELOCs and credit cards revolve the way they really do. See it: Mortgage on vs paid off.
  • Life insurance. Term, whole and universal policies carry their premiums, cash value and tax-free death benefit. See it: Life insurance on the plan.

Taxes

  • Federal tax. Every projected year gets a full federal return, including capital gains, AMT, the NIIT and the tax on Social Security. See it: Benefits taxation vs payroll FICA.
  • State tax. All 50 states are modeled, with local taxes and retirement-income exclusions. See it: Compare a move, in the app tour.
  • IRMAA. Medicare surcharges are worked out from your income two years earlier, the way Medicare does it. See it: MAGI cliffs (IRMAA / NIIT).
  • ACA cliffs. The premium credit follows your income as a percent of the poverty line, including the cliff at 400%. See it: %FPL: the number under the subsidy cliffs.
  • Cliff Radar. It shows how close each year sits to each tax cliff, so you can see one coming. See it: Cliff radar.
  • Tax savings. Try a Roth conversion, a move or a different withdrawal order, and see the tax it saves year by year. See it: Tax-account order vs pro-rata.
  • Estate planning. Federal and state estate and inheritance tax are modeled, and you see what your heirs would receive after tax.
  • Wealth tax what-if. You can try a hypothetical wealth tax with its own rate and exemption. It stays off unless you turn it on. See it: A wealth tax on your plan.

Income and Social Security

  • Social Security. Start from your statement and compare every claim age, with early reductions, delayed credits and cost-of-living increases. See it: Claim timing and longevity.
  • Trust fund shortfall. You can plan for benefits cut to 83% from 2034, or for the cut and year you choose. See it: Will Social Security be there?.
  • Pensions and annuities. Each one pays a monthly amount from the age you choose, with a cost-of-living increase. A pension you take as a lump sum goes in as a one-time payment in the year you take it.
  • Income and spending. About 25 guided items cover wages, rental income, spending in phases, healthcare, college and long-term care. See it: Healthcare expense phases.

Withdrawals

  • Roth conversions. Convert a fixed amount or fill a tax bracket, in the years you choose. See it: Roth conversion in the window.
  • RMDs. Required minimum distributions follow SECURE 2.0, including the new start ages. See it: Forced traditional RMDs.
  • Inherited IRAs. The 10-year rule and the stretch for eligible beneficiaries are both modeled. See it: Inheriting an IRA: the 10-year rule.
  • Withdrawal order. Draw pro-rata, in tax-account order, or along a glide path that rebalances as you age. See it: Tax-account order vs pro-rata.
  • Withdrawal strategies. Bengen, Guyton-Klinger guardrails and four more rules run on your own plan, side by side. See it: Guardrail for me.

Monte Carlo and risk

  • Monte Carlo simulation. In this professional-level feature, each holding gets its own random return every year, and those returns move together the way real assets do. See it: Correlation and diversification.
  • Historical crashes. Replay the crash of 1929, 1973, 2000 or 2008 starting in any year you choose. See it: Timing a crash.
  • Interest-rate shocks. See what a rise in rates does to your plan. See it: Interest rates: the price of money in your plan.
  • Custom growth rates. Keep the market assumptions that ship with the app, each with its source, or set your own. See it: Growth rates: the system's, and yours.
  • Success rate. Choose how success is defined, and read your plan's risks in plain language. See it: What does success mean?.

Multi-currency

  • 14 currencies. Each item is simulated in its own currency and shown in yours. See it: Why every amount carries a currency.
  • Currency risk. Turn on moving exchange rates, and the forecast carries the risk of what you own abroad. See it: Currency risk in the forecast.
  • Live exchange rates. One click fetches today's rates.

Scenarios

  • What-if scenarios. Try a change without touching your plan, and every chart and table shows the original beside the new version. See it: Working fast.
  • Side-by-side comparisons. When you edit the plan itself, the forecast summary keeps the result from before your edit beside the new one, so you can see what your change moved.
  • Relocation. Click a state on the map and see your plan as if you lived there. See it: Compare a move, in the app tour.

Charts and reports

  • Cash flow. For any year, you see every dollar, traced from where it came from to where it went. See it: Reading the charts and tables.
  • Household overview. Everything you own and will earn is shown against everything you owe and will spend, on one screen. See it: Working fast.
  • Milestones. Retirements, claim ages and sales are marked on the timeline, and one click takes you to that year. See it: The years that change the rules.
  • Yearly tax return. You can open each year's return and see where your income falls in the brackets. See it: Cliff radar.
  • Today's dollars. One switch shows the whole plan in today's dollars or in future dollars. See it: Prices on your plan.
  • Copy and export. Tables copy out as text, and charts copy out as images.

Navigation

  • Search everything. Type a word such as roth or irmaa and jump to every place it appears in the app. See it: Working fast.
  • Keyboard shortcuts. One key opens each section and tool, and k, m and b make large amounts quick to type. See it: Working fast.
  • Hover for sources. Rest the pointer on any number to see the plan item behind it. See it: Working fast.

What it models See every feature →

  • What you actually own. Each holding has its own expected return and volatility, and holdings move together the way real assets do. A portfolio is not an average.
  • A real tax return for every projected year. Federal brackets, state tax, the character of each kind of income, and the thresholds that bite: Medicare IRMAA, the net investment income tax, required minimum distributions.
  • Every dollar, traced. For any year you can see the balance sheet, the cash flows, and how the one became the next. If a number looks wrong, you can find out why.
  • What-ifs beside the plan. Retire earlier, move states, convert to Roth, or sell the house. Each change is layered on your plan and compared with it.
  • Withdrawal strategies. These include guardrail approaches that adjust spending to how the portfolio is doing.

Who it is for

People who already take their own planning seriously. You may use a planning tool today. You have questions a simple calculator cannot hold, such as when to convert to Roth, how a withdrawal changes a Medicare premium, or whether a move across state lines pays. You want a tool that models your situation rather than a rule of thumb about it.

Tutilis forecasts. It is not a budgeting app, it is not a phone app, and it is not financial advice. The decisions stay yours.
